## Abstract
The ropelength of a knot is the minimum length required to tie it. Computational upper bounds have previously been computed for every prime knot with up to 11 crossings. Here, we present ropelength measurements for the 2176 knots with 12 crossings, of which 1288 are alternating and 888 are non-alternating. We report on the distribution of ropelengths within and between crossing numbers, as well as the space writhe of the tight knot configurations. It was previously established that tight alternating knots have a "quantized" space writhe close to a multiple of 4/7. Our data supports this for 12-crossing alternating knots and we find that non-alternating knots also show evidence of writhe quantization, falling near integer or half-integer multiples of 4/3, depending on the parity of the crossing number. Finally, we examine correlations between geometric properties and topological invariants of tight knots, finding that the ropelength is positively correlated with hyperbolic volume and its correlates, and that the space writhe is positively correlated with the Rasmussen s invariant.

## Notation and conventions
- **Ropelength convention = length / tube RADIUS.** p. 1: "if a knot is tied in an incompressible, inextensible unit-radius tube with the knot along its axis, the ideal configuration of the tube has the minimum possible ratio of contour length to tube radius that respects a no-overlap constraint [1, 2]. The contour length of an ideal knot is known as the ropelength". (Consistent with trefoil 32.74 in Table I and Hopf necklace 6·(4π+4) on p. 7.)
- **"Ropelength" in this paper = computed upper bound.** p. 2: "Throughout the rest of this manuscript, we use the term ropelength to refer to the upper bounds on ropelength that we measured computationally. The "true" ropelength of all knots is unproven except for the unknot and certain classes of chained Hopf links [1]."
- **Writhe.** p. 2: "We also use the term "writhe" and "space writhe" to refer to the space writhe of the ideal configuration of the knot." Defined p. 2, Eq. (1): Wr = (1/4π) ∫_K ∫_K d r⃗1 × d r⃗2 · (r⃗1 − r⃗2)/|r⃗1 − r⃗2|³, "the average writhe of the knot projected over every direction in space"; for discrete knots computed as a double sum over segment pairs as in Klenin–Langowski [11] (p. 3).
- **Average crossing number** p. 3, Eq. (2): ACN = (1/4π) ∫_K ∫_K |r̂ · d r⃗1 × d r⃗2| / |r⃗1 − r⃗2|², "where r̂ is the unit vector along r⃗2 − r⃗1".
- C = crossing number (in fits, Table III and p. 3). "A"/"N" suffix in Table II = alternating / non-alternating.
- Residual writhe (Fig. 5): deviation of space writhe from its "quantized" value.
- Absolute values of writhe are used in Fig. 4, "equivalent to choosing one stereoisomer for chiral knots" (p. 5).

## Key equations and quantitative claims outside numbered statements
All ropelengths are length/radius, and are numerical upper bounds from Ridgerunner unless stated.
- p. 1: Dataset: 2176 knots with 12 crossings: 1288 alternating, 888 non-alternating.
- p. 3 (numerical): population mean 12-crossing ropelength 102.95 (s.d. 5.51); alternating mean 106.989 (s.d. 1.49); non-alternating mean 97.082 (s.d. 3.61).
- p. 3 (numerical): "non-alternating knots have about 90% the mean ropelength as alternating knots with the same crossing number, but at least twice the standard deviation."
- p. 3: Pearson correlation of ACN with ropelength: 0.30 (alternating), 0.81 (non-alternating).
- p. 3 (numerical fit, heuristic): best-fit power law exponent of ropelength vs crossing number (3–12 crossings) previously 0.81 ± 0.03 [8], "updated to 0.83 ± 0.02". "These fits are unweighted to avoid bias towards crossing numbers with more knots, and the uncertainties are standard errors on the fit parameters." "These fits should be treated as a heuristic way to estimate ropelength given a crossing number, but caution should be taken when making inferences about the ropelength scaling of the ensemble of all knots." Full fits in Table III.
- p. 3 (quoted theorem): proven lower bound for ropelength "currently 34.7 [18]" (Diao 2003).
- p. 3 (quoted theorem + numerical constraint): Diao [9] proved alternating-knot ropelength ≥ linear lower bound "with a slope of at least 1/59.5". The 11a367 torus knot has ropelength at most 89.61, "meaning that slope cannot exceed 8.15". "However, the supercoiled configurations of Huh et al. [19] set a stronger bound of 7.63."
- p. 3 (quoted theorem + numerical constraint): "It is known that non-alternating knots must have a ropelength greater than 1.1C^{3/4}, with the true coefficient likely higher than 1.1." 10_124 torus knot gives upper bound 12.64 on that coefficient; 12n725 gives 12.74 ("although this may be reduced by annealing with more vertices"); prior work [8]: 1023-crossing T(32,33) constrains this to 10.76. ⚠ The paper does not give a citation for the 1.1C^{3/4} bound in this sentence, and says "non-alternating knots" (the C^{3/4} bound is generally stated for all knots; transcribed as printed).
- p. 3 (quoted): Pieranski–Przybyl [12]: majority of ideal alternating knots ≤10 crossings have writhe within 0.1 of a multiple of 4/7, greatest deviation 0.25. Trefoil predicted writhe 24/7; most precise measurement [20] ≈ 23.92/7.
- p. 3 / p. 5 (numerical): two-parameter sweep minimizing variance of frac(Wr/A + B) for non-alternating 12-crossing knots gives A = 1.3434, B = 0.5253; interpreted as "quasi-quantization at half-integer multiples of 4/3, or odd multiples of 2/3".
- p. 5 (numerical, 8–11-crossing non-alternating, using Gilbert's ideal configurations, 512 vertices from Fourier coefficients): even crossing number → writhe near an odd multiple of 2/3; odd crossing number → near an even multiple of 2/3; within each crossing number, clusters separated by 4/3.
- p. 5: 8_20 predicted writhe 2 (Stasiak [21]); three independent measurements [2,6,7] give ideal writhe 1.95 or 1.96.
- p. 5 (numerical): residual writhe distributions at 12 crossings: alternating about 4/7: mean −.01, s.d. 0.14; non-alternating about 4/3: mean .50, s.d. 0.19. Pieranski–Przybyl s.d. ≈ 0.09 (alternating ≤10 crossings). Uniform null on [0,1]: s.d. 0.29.
- p. 5–6: Pearson correlation ropelength vs hyperbolic volume: 0.35 (alt.), 0.74 (non-alt.); ropelength vs |Alexander(−1)|: 0.39, 0.55; "for example 0.95 for 10-crossing alternating knots" (ropelength vs volume, lower crossings).
- p. 6: ideal writhe vs Rasmussen s and Ozsváth–Szabó τ: correlation 0.96 (12 crossings); "present for 11-crossing knots, but does not appear significant for 10." "We have no formal or heuristic explanation for this."
- p. 6 + ref. [26] p. 9: torus knots at 63 crossings: T(9,8) Alexander value 9, ropelength 260.0; T(21,4) Alexander 21, ropelength 275.5; T(63,2) Alexander −63, ropelength "likely between 486 and 532 [19]"; ropelength and |Alexander(−1)| ordered. 4320 crossings: 10 torus knots. Torus knots with 5–11 crossings each have smallest ropelength in their class.
- p. 7 (numerical calibration): 12-crossing Hopf chain necklace of six loops, known ropelength 6·(4π + 4) ≈ 99.4 [1]; Ridgerunner with 20 vertices/link (120 total) reaches 100.63 (1.2% above); 12 vertices/link reaches 102.24 (2.9% above). "Based on vertex count alone, it would be safe to estimate at least 1% ropelength excess."
- p. 7: extra 2000 steps on largest-ropelength knots affected only second or third decimal digit. KnotPlot pre-treatment of outliers "reduced the population average by about 0.5."
- p. 7–8: conclusion: "these ropelength measurements are likely at least 1% overestimated".
- p. 8: 9,988 knots with 13 crossings, "the first crossing number at which most knots are non-alternating."

## Methods / algorithms
1. (p. 2) Initial coordinates: Cartesian coordinates of 12-crossing knots generated by Se-Goo Kim, from KnotInfo [15]. Mean 125 vertices (range 88–160). Converted to Ridgerunner input.
2. (p. 2) Tightening: Ridgerunner (Cantarella; constrained gradient optimization [6]). Common options for all knots: annealed with an equilateralization force for 25,000 steps or until residual gradient 0.1; then without equilateralization force for 12,000 steps or until residual 0.001.
3. (p. 2) Knots stuck in deep local minima: imported into KnotPlot [16], Coulombic repulsion plus tangential tightening force to symmetrize and pre-tighten, then back into Ridgerunner.
4. (p. 2) Verification: Alexander polynomial evaluated at −1 compared between initial, final, and tabulated values. Six initial configurations incorrect (since corrected on KnotInfo); two knots changed topology during tightening; these eight were re-sketched, annealed in KnotPlot, re-run. "It remains a rare possibility that a knot is transformed into one with the same Alexander polynomial."
5. (p. 2–3) Computed space writhe (Eq. 1) and ACN (Eq. 2) as discrete double sums. For 8–11 crossings, Brian Gilbert's Fourier coefficients used to generate tight configurations with 512 vertices.
6. (p. 3–5) Writhe periodicity: two-parameter sweep minimizing variance of fractional part of (Wr/A + B).
7. Data: ropelengths, writhes, ACN, and coordinates on Harvard Dataverse, https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/6AXP61 [17].

## Stated limitations, open problems, future work
- p. 3: Fits are heuristic; "caution should be taken when making inferences about the ropelength scaling of the ensemble of all knots." 12-crossing knots "typically not complex enough to interface with the proven lower bound".
- p. 3: "The quantization of writhe is likely not absolute"; "One should not necessarily assume that a knot with a larger residual writhe is not fully tight."
- p. 5: "It is possible that there may be exceptions to the non-alternating half-integer rule, as indicated by the top three points of Figure 4 and the rightmost bin of Figure 5." Enhanced shrinking of large-residual knots "did not prove a fruitful strategy for reducing the mean measured ropelength."
- p. 6: Reduced volume–ropelength correlation at higher crossing number "may be indicative that this trend is a property of simple knots and vanishes as knots become asymptotically complex, or just an indication that it is more difficult to converge". Writhe–Rasmussen correlation: "We have no formal or heuristic explanation for this."
- p. 6–7 (Looseness considerations): heuristic reasons for overestimation: 12-crossing point above trendline in all fits; 12A only large sample with positive skew; weak positive correlation between alternating ropelength and residual writhe; three causes: insufficient vertices, insufficient runtime, unfavorable initial conditions (the last "likely the biggest contributor").
- p. 8: "Unless the algorithms or available hardware are significantly improved, we do not recommend an exhaustive ropelength for higher crossing numbers." Suggests a subset of a few hundred ≥13-crossing knots; "an approach combining Coulomb repulsive molecular dynamics and a traditional minimization algorithm may be more efficient".
