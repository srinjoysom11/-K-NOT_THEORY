## Abstract
In this paper we are concerned with questions about the knottedness of a closed curve of given length embedded in Z³. What is the probability that such a randomly chosen embedding is knotted? What is the probability that the embedding contains a particular knot? What is the expected complexity of the knot? To what extent can these questions also be answered for a graph of a given homeomorphism type? We use a pattern theorem due to Kesten [12] to prove that almost all embeddings in Z³ of a sufficiently long closed curve contain any given knot. We introduce the idea of a good measure of knot complexity. This is a function F which maps the set of equivalence classes of embeddings into [0, ∞). The F measure of the unknot is zero, and, generally speaking, the more complex the prime knot decomposition of a given knot type, the greater its F measure. We prove that the average value of F diverges to infinity as the length (n) of the embedding goes to infinity, at least linearly in n. One example of a good measure of knot complexity is crossing number. Finally we consider similar questions for embeddings of graphs. We show that for a fixed homeomorphism type, as the number of edges n goes to infinity, almost all embeddings are knotted if the homeomorphism type does not contain a cut edge. We prove a weaker result in the case that the homeomorphism type contains at least one cut edge and at least one cycle.

## Notation and conventions
- (p. 2 [76]) PL category throughout; τ = homeomorphism type of a finite connected 1-complex; knot of τ in S³(R³) = locally flat PL embedding k: G → S³(R³); embeddings unoriented, ambient space oriented; K = equivalence class under orientation-preserving homeomorphism of (S³, k); 𝒦(τ) = classes. Crossing number C(K) = minimum number of crossings over all diagrams of all representatives; N_min(τ) = min_K C(K) over embedding classes of τ (Eq. 2-1).
- (p. 3 [77]) Knot product # (associative, commutative on 𝒦, (𝒦,#) a commutative semigroup with unknot as identity); nK = n-fold product K#…#K (⚠ OCR prints "nK" for the n-fold sum; used in this paper as K^n elsewhere); prime knot = cannot factor into two non-trivial knots.
- (p. 3) Z³ simple cubic lattice; step = directed edge between adjacent lattice points; **n-step self-avoiding walk (n-SAW)** from x_0 = (n+1)-tuple of distinct lattice points (x_0,…,x_n), x_i, x_{i+1} adjacent; rooted if x_0 = 0; c_n = number of rooted n-SAWs (distinct as (n+1)-tuples); connective constant κ: 0 < lim n^{-1} log c_n = inf_{n>0} n^{-1} log c_n = κ < ∞ (Eq. 2-2, Hammersley [6]). **n-step self-avoiding circuit (n-SAC)** = (n−1)-SAW whose first and last vertices are unit distance apart; the 2n SACs obtained from cyclic permutations and reversal form one **n-step self-avoiding polygon (n-SAP)**; two n-SAPs equivalent if one is a translate of the other (so polygons counted up to translation); p_n = number of inequivalent n-SAPs, p_n = 0 for n odd (n even in all statements); lim n^{-1} log p_n = κ (Eq. 2-3, Hammersley [7]).
- (p. 4 [78]) A k-SAW P *appears* at step i of an n-SAW α if (x_i − x_i = 0, x_{i+1} − x_i, …, x_{i+k} − x_i) = P; appears r times if at r distinct steps with the vertices of any two copies disjoint or terminal vertex of one = initial vertex of the next. **Kesten pattern**: P for which some SAW β contains P three times; equivalently [16] there is a SAW ω on which P occurs and a cube D such that ω lies in D with endpoints in ∂D. For any Kesten pattern and any r there is an n and an n-SAW containing P r times.
- Lattice neighbourhood N(α) = union of the 3-cells dual to the vertices of α (each occupied site at the barycentre of a dual 3-cube = "excluded volume"); α′ = (n−2)-SAW obtained by deleting the first and last steps; if N(α′) is a 3-ball and the ball pair (N(α′), N(α′)∩α) is knotted, α contains a **knotted arc** (local knot, **tight knot**) (p. 4).
- Good measure of knot complexity: Definition 3.1 (below). Embeddings of graph τ counted by size n = number of occupied lattice vertices, up to translation; g(n,τ) = number of embeddings (p. 8 [82]); top/bottom vertex defined recursively by maximal/minimal coordinates; top plane, top line (p. 9). 𝒢 = circle graph ∪ finite connected graphs with no degree-2 vertices; 𝒢_6 ⊂ 𝒢 those with maximum degree ≤ 6. Edge-separable = has a cut edge.

## Numbered statements
- **Theorem 2.1 (p. 3) [quoted, Schubert [23]].** If K ∈ 𝒦, then K = K_1 # … # K_n, where each K_i is a prime knot, and the factorization is unique up to order of the prime factors.
- **Theorem 2.2 (Kesten's Pattern Theorem) (p. 4) [quoted, [12]].** Let P be any Kesten pattern. There exists a positive number ζ = ζ_P such that if c_n(P, ζ) denotes the number of rooted n-SAWs in which P appears at most ⌊nζ⌋ times, then limsup_{n→∞} n^{-1} log c_n(P, ζ) < κ. (Eq. 2-4; "for sufficiently large n, the pattern P appears at least ⌊nζ⌋ times in all but exponentially few rooted n-SAWs".)
- **Theorem 2.3 (p. 4) [quoted, [25] Sumners–Whittington].** Let P be any Kesten pattern. There exists a positive number λ = λ_P such that if p_n(P, λ) denotes the number of n-SAPs in which P appears at most ⌊nλ⌋ times, then limsup_{n→∞} n^{-1} log p_n(P, λ) < κ. (Eq. 2-5.)
- **Theorem 2.4 (p. 5 [79]) — proved here.** Every knot type is represented by a Kesten pattern which forms a tight knot. *Proof idea (paper):* for the trefoil, the following 18-step walk is a Kesten pattern forming a tight (+)trefoil knotted arc: {û_1, û_1, û_2, û_3, û_3, −û_2, −û_2, −û_3, −û_1, −û_3, −û_3, û_2, û_2, û_3, û_3, −û_2, û_1, û_1} (û_1,û_2,û_3 unit vectors of a right-handed XYZ system; read from the page image). For general K: take a representative with a point at infinity so that it becomes a knotted arc with both ends along the X-axis; project to a square-lattice immersion in Z=0 inside a square with vertices (±d, ±d), d even; realise underpasses by detours into the Z = −1 plane; then lengthen the walk to "drape" over the enclosing square in plane Z = 1 (alternate up/down traversals) so that the lattice neighbourhood becomes a 3-ball ("a pancake with a pretzel decoration"), giving a tight knot representative which is a Kesten pattern.
- **Corollary 2.5 (p. 6 [80]).** Let K denote a given knot type; then there exists a positive number ζ_K such that, for sufficiently large n, the knot K appears at least ⌊nζ_K⌋ times in the prime factorization of all but exponentially few SAPs of length n on Z³.
- **Definition 3.1 (p. 6).** A good measure of knot complexity is a function F: 𝒦 → [0, ∞) which satisfies: (i) F(unknot) = 0; (ii) there exists K ∈ 𝒦 such that F(nK # L) ≥ nF(K) > 0 for all L ∈ 𝒦.
- **Corollary 3.2 (p. 6).** If F is a good measure of knot complexity, let K be a knot which satisfies part (ii) of the above definition for F. Then there exists a positive integer n_K such that for sufficiently large n > n_K, all but exponentially few n-SAPs have F-complexity which exceeds F(K)(n_K^{-1} n − 1). (Checked on image.) *Proof (paper):* choose ζ_K as in Cor. 2.5 and n_K with ⌊(n_K−1)ζ_K⌋ = 0, ⌊n_K ζ_K⌋ = 1; all but exponentially few K′ are of the form ⌊nζ_K⌋K # L, so F(K′) ≥ ⌊nζ_K⌋F(K) > F(K)(n_K^{-1} n − 1).
- **Theorem 3.3 (p. 6).** The following are good measures of knot complexity: number of prime factors, genus, bridge number minus one, span of any non-trivial Laurent knot polynomial, log(order), crossing number, unknotting number, minor index, braid index minus one. *Proof idea:* all are non-trivial invariants, some additive on products; where additivity cannot be proved use that the + trefoil 3_1 satisfies F(n3_1 # L) ≥ nF(3_1) > 0. Facts used (pp. 7–8): g(K_1#K_2) = g(K_1)+g(K_2), g(3_1)=1; b(K_1#K_2) = b(K_1)+b(K_2)−1, b(3_1)=2 (b(unknot)=1); knot polynomials multiplicative, span of Jones polynomial of 3_1 = 3 (V = t^{-1}+t^{-3}−t^{-4}); order = |Δ_K(−1)|, order(n3_1) = 3^n; Theorem 3.5; μ(K_1#K_2) ≤ … via Thm 3.7; braid index β(3_1)=2.
- **Conjecture 3.4 (p. 7).** C(K_1 # K_2) = C(K_1) + C(K_2) for all K_1, K_2 (crossing number additivity). Holds for weakly alternating knots (products of prime alternating knots) [11, 20, 27].
- **Theorem 3.5 (p. 7) [quoted, [11, 20, 27]].** For any knot type K ∈ 𝒦, C(K) ≥ span(V_K(t)); equality is achieved if and only if K is weakly alternating. (Hence C(nK # L) ≥ n span(V_K) + span(V_L) ≥ 3n for K = 3_1.)
- **Conjecture 3.6 (p. 8).** μ(K_1 # K_2) = μ(K_1) + μ(K_2) for all K_1, K_2 (unknotting number is believed additive on knot products) (μ = unknotting number).
- **Theorem 3.7 (p. 8) [quoted, [21] Nakanishi].** For any K ∈ 𝒦, 0 ≤ m(K) ≤ μ(K) (m = minor index of the Alexander matrix). Hence 0 ≤ n ≤ m(nK#L) ≤ μ(nK#L) for K = 3_1.
- **Inequality (3-1) (p. 8) [quoted, [19, 29]].** The braid index satisfies β(K) − 1 ≥ ½ span_l(P_K) where P_K(l,m) is the HOMFLY polynomial (checked on image). Since span_l(P_{3_1}) = 2, braid index minus one is a good measure.
- **Lemma 4.1 (p. 9 [83]).** (A) There is an embedding of τ in Z³ if and only if τ ∈ 𝒢_6 (i.e. there exists m > 0 such that g(m,τ) > 0 iff τ ∈ 𝒢_6). (B) Furthermore, there exists an embedding in which at least one vertex (exactly one vertex if the branch ends in a vertex of degree one) and at most one edge of every branch lies in the top plane and the vertices of degree one lie in the top line. In particular, if τ is a tree then at least one vertex of every branch and at most one edge of every branch lies in the top line. *Proved* by induction on cyclomatic index (trees by induction on branch points, Figs 3–5; cut an edge to remove a cycle, Figs 6–7).
- **Theorem 4.2 (p. 11 [85]).** For τ ∈ 𝒢_6, 0 < lim_{n→∞} n^{-1} log g(n, τ) = κ < ∞ (Eq. 4-2) — the connective constant is independent of τ; "depending on τ, n may be required to be only even, otherwise the limit is independent of τ". *Proved:* lower bound from g(n,τ) ≥ g(n−m−2, π) (Eq. 4-3) by inserting a polygon at the top vertex of a fixed embedding; upper bound by embedding branches independently using c_m ≤ e^{(m+1)κ(m+1)^{...}} type bound from Hammersley–Welsh [8] (Eqs 4-5–4-7).
- **Theorem 4.3 (p. 12 [87]).** Let g(n, τ, P) be the number of embeddings in G(n, τ) such that the embedding does not contain the Kesten pattern P. Then limsup_{n→∞} n^{-1} log g(n, τ, P) < κ (Eq. 4-8).
- **Theorem 4.4 (p. 13 [87]).** For sufficiently large n, given a non-edge-separable graph type τ ∈ 𝒢_6, all but exponentially few embeddings of size n of τ in Z³ are knotted. *Proof (paper):* take pattern P = T^q (q suitably translated copies of the tight trefoil) with q = ⌈(1+1/3)N_min(τ)⌉ (as printed, garbled; ⚠ exact q read from OCR "q = ⌈(1+⅓)N_min(τ)⌉"); a knotted cycle with q trefoil factors forces ≥ 3q crossings > N_min(τ), hence knotted.
- **Lemma 4.5 (p. 14 [88]).** For any graph τ ∈ 𝒢_6 there exists an embedding for which the top vertex is contained in the k-th branch and the bottom vertex is contained in the j-th branch for any j, k. (Checked on image.)
- **Lemma 4.6 (p. 14).** For every α ∈ (0,1) and for any set of branches k_1, …, k_r where r is less than the total number of branches in τ ∈ 𝒢_6, lim_{n→∞} n^{-1} log g^α_{k_1,…,k_r}(n,τ) = κ (Eq. 4-12), where as in Theorem 4.2, depending on τ, n may be restricted to even numbers; g^α_{k_1…k_r}(n,τ) = number of embeddings in which the r branches k_1…k_r contain altogether ⌊αn⌋ vertices (checked on image).
- **Lemma 4.7 (p. 15 [89]).** limsup_{n→∞} n^{-1} log g^α_{k_1,…,k_r}(n, τ, P) < κ, where g^α(…,P) counts embeddings with n vertices not containing the Kesten pattern P in branches k_1,…,k_r (Eq. 4-16).
- **Theorem 4.8 (p. 15).** For sufficiently large n, all but exponentially few of the embeddings in Z³ of a graph τ ∈ 𝒢_6 having a circuit containing a fixed positive fraction of the edges in the embedding are knotted. (Weaker result for edge-separable graphs containing a cycle.)
- **Theorem 4.9 (p. 16 [90]).** Let F ∈ {crossing number, unknotting number, number of prime factors, genus, bridge number minus one, span of any non-trivial Laurent knot polynomial, log(order), minor index, braid index minus one}, and let τ ∈ 𝒢_6 be a non-edge-separable graph type. Then there exists a positive constant α_F such that for sufficiently large n all but exponentially few embeddings of τ of size n have F-complexity which exceeds α_F n. A similar result holds for graphs τ ∈ 𝒢_6 having a circuit containing a fixed positive fraction of the edges in the embedding. (Complexity of a graph embedding class κ defined by F(κ) = min over generating sets {κ_1,…,κ_c} of H_1 of Σ F(κ_i), Eq. 4-20, for F other than crossing number and unknotting number; crossing number and unknotting number defined directly, unknotting number minimised over all unknotted embeddings, p. 16.)

- Eq. (2-2), (2-3) (p. 3): connective constant κ = lim n^{-1} log c_n = inf n^{-1} log c_n; lim n^{-1} log p_n = κ (Hammersley, quoted).
- p. 8: every graph with max valence > 6 has no embedding in Z³ (Lemma 4.1(A)); Section 4 stratifies embeddings by number n of occupied lattice vertices.
- p. 13 (before Thm 4.4): K_5 and K_{3,3} non-planar; every embedding of K_6 contains a pair of homologically linked cycles [2]; K_7 intrinsically knotted [2]; K_{4k+3} intrinsically chiral [4] (quoted background; "unknotted" embedding class of τ defined as the class realising N_min(τ)).
- p. 9 (Section 3 remark): in Monte Carlo simulations of random knotting the order |Δ(−1)| is the invariant of choice, since it needs a determinant of size n² (n crossings); log(order) growing at least linearly implies efficient detection (heuristic comment).

## Methods / algorithms
Pure-math proof strategy:
1. Section 2: Kesten's pattern theorem (walks) ⇒ pattern theorem for SAPs via equal connective constants; Theorem 2.4 constructs, for every knot type, a tight-knot Kesten pattern (via lattice immersion of an arc, underpass detours into Z=−1, and a "draping" extension in Z=1 making N(α) a 3-ball) ⇒ Corollary 2.5 (almost all long SAPs contain any given knot ≳ ζn times).
2. Section 3: good measures of complexity F are ≥ linear in the multiplicity of a fixed knot ⇒ Cor. 3.2; Theorem 3.3 verifies that standard invariants are good measures (additivity or the 3_1 argument).
3. Section 4: Lemma 4.1 gives embeddings of any τ ∈ 𝒢_6 with top/bottom control; Theorem 4.2 concatenation injections show g(n,τ) ~ e^{κn}; Theorem 4.3 pattern theorem for graphs; Thm 4.4 pattern T^q forces crossing number > N_min(τ); Lemmas 4.5–4.7 and Thm 4.8 handle edge-separable graphs with a long circuit; Thm 4.9 extends to complexity measures.
