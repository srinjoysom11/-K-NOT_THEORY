## Abstract
Coarse-grained (lattice-) models have a long tradition in aiding efforts to decipher the physical or biological complexity of proteins. Despite the simplicity of these models, however, numerical simulations are often computationally very demanding and the quest for efficient algorithms is as old as the models themselves. Expanding on our previous work [T. Wüst and D. P. Landau, Phys. Rev. Lett. 102, 178101 (2009)], we present a complete picture of a Monte Carlo method based on Wang-Landau sampling in combination with efficient trial moves (pull, bond-rebridging and pivot moves) which is particularly suited to the study of models such as the hydrophobic-polar (HP) lattice model of protein folding. With this generic and fully blind Monte Carlo procedure, all currently known putative ground states for the most difficult benchmark HP sequences could be found. For most sequences we could also determine the entire energy density of states and, together with suitably designed structural observables, explore the thermodynamics and intricate folding behavior in the virtually inaccessible low-temperature regime. We analyze the differences between random and protein-like heteropolymers for sequence lengths up to 500 residues. Our approach is powerful both in terms of robustness and speed, yet flexible and simple enough for the study of many related problems in protein folding.

## Notation and conventions
- HP model proteins: isolated self-avoiding walks (SAWs) on a regular lattice, each site a monomer (H or P); only square (2D) and simple cubic (3D) lattices (p. 2). N = number of monomers (SAW has length N − 1). Energy E = number of non-bonded nearest-neighbour H–H contacts, each with energy −ε (p. 2). Ground-state energies quoted as E_min (integer, in units of ε, negative).
- g(E) = energy density of states (DOS); Z_N(T) = Σ_{ω∈Ω_N} e^{−E/kT} = Σ_E g(E) e^{−E/kT} (Eq. 1, p. 2).
- f = modification factor (initially f = e¹), p = flatness criterion (0 < p < 1), f_final threshold; ln f_final used as the accuracy parameter (p. 3).
- t_hit = time between independent hits of a ground state, defined as the time of a "round trip" in which a zero-energy conformation must have been visited at least once between two consecutive ground-state hits; t_DOS = time until convergence of the DOS over the whole energy interval [E_min, 0] (p. 7). WLS = this Wang-Landau sampling method; WLS timings are the interquartile mean of 20 independent WL runs (p. 7).
- nPERMis = improved pruned-enriched Rosenbluth method (Ref. 18); FRESS = fragment regrowth Monte Carlo via energy-guided sequential sampling (Ref. 23) (p. 7).
- Rg = root-mean-squared radius of gyration (Eq. 9); τ = tortuosity (Eq. 10-11) (p. 10-11).
- Move names: pull move, bond-rebridging (connectivity-altering) move, pivot move (p. 3-5).

## Key equations and quantitative claims outside numbered statements
Equations
- Eq. (1) (p. 2): Z_N(T) = Σ_ω e^{−E/kT} = Σ_E g(E) e^{−E/kT}.
- Eq. (2) (p. 3): WL acceptance P(A→B) = min{1, g(E_A)/g(E_B)}. After each trial move g(E_t) ← f·g(E_t) and H(E_t) ← H(E_t)+1 (t = B if accepted, else A). When H(E) ≥ p·⟨H⟩ for all (visited) E, f ← √f, H reset to 0; stop when f < f_final (≈ 1). Initially g(E) = 1 ∀E, f = e¹.
- Eq. (3) (p. 4, image-checked): max n_pull = (N − 2) × 4(d − 1) + 2 × (2d − 1 + (2d − 2)²); first term internal pull moves, second term end pull moves; d = lattice dimension.
- Eq. (4) (p. 5, text layer): NMSE = ((ḡ − ĝ)/ĝ)² + (1/(N−1)) Σ_{i=1}^{N} ((g_i − ḡ)/ḡ)² (bias term + variance term), ĝ = exact DOS, ḡ = averaged DOS and g_i = individual DOS at a given E, N = 200 independent runs.
- Eqs. (5), (6) (p. 6): U(T) = (1/Z(T)) Σ_E E g(E) e^{−E/kT} ≡ ⟨E⟩; C(T) = ∂U/∂T = (⟨E²⟩ − ⟨E⟩²)/(kT²).
- Eqs. (7), (8) (p. 7): ⟨Q⟩(T) = (1/Z(T)) Σ_E e^{−E/kT} g(E) ⟨Q⟩_E with ⟨Q⟩_E = Σ_Q Q H(E,Q)/Σ_Q H(E,Q).
- Eq. (9) (p. 10): Rg = [(1/N) Σ_i (r_i − r_cm)²]^{1/2}.
- Eq. (10), (11) (p. 10-11): τ = [ (1/(N−2)) Σ_{i=1}^{N−2} (s_i − s̄)² ]^{1/2}, s_i = Σ_{j=1}^{i} r_{j,j+1} × r_{j,j+2} for 1 ≤ i ≤ N−2 (cross product of consecutive bond vectors: left (−1) / right (+1) turn, collinear 0; "winding walk"; τ invariant to direction of summation). Quantitative claims
- p. 1: HP 103mer has 59 energy levels, g(E) spans > 50 orders of magnitude; the corresponding interacting SAW (all H) has 139 energies, g(E) range 38 orders of magnitude.
- p. 2: finding the lowest energy conformation for a given HP sequence is NP-complete (Ref. 24).
- p. 3: new energy found ⇒ marked "visited", g(E_new) set to g_min (min of g among visited energies), histogram reset; flatness checked among visited energies only, with long intervals between checks (e.g. every 10⁶ MC moves).
- p. 3: combination of bond-rebridging and pull moves gave overall speed-up in WL convergence up to a factor 3 vs pull moves only; ground states of some HP sequences would not have been found at all without both. Pivot moves further improved sampling for most sequences; prerequisite for N ≫ 100.
- p. 3: on average less than 10 monomers are displaced during a pull move.
- p. 4-5: trial-and-error selection (constant probability, invalid moves rejected and g, H of the old state updated) outperforms the enumeration approach by 1-2 orders of magnitude in speed without loss of accuracy.
- p. 5: move fractions 75% pull, 23% bond-rebridging, 2% pivot; energy-dependent fractions tried, no significant gain for these sequences.
- p. 5: bit-table lattice of size L^d, L = N + 2, periodic boundaries; self-avoidance/neighbour queries O(d) instead of O(d×N); energy change computed only when ΔN < N/4, cost O(d×ΔN).
- p. 5: RNG Mersenne Twister MT19937 (speed); RANLUX for cross-validation; both from GNU Scientific Library.
- p. 5-6: tests of accuracy on two short HP sequences with exactly known DOS (20mer in 2D [Ref. 25], 14mer in 3D [Ref. 50]), N = 200 independent runs; NMSE falls with decreasing f and increasing p (Fig. 2); with ln f_final = 10⁻¹⁰ and p = 0.999 very high accuracy over whole energy range; with ln f_final = 10⁻⁸ and p = 0.8 DOS deviates "max. a few percent" from exact; bias term is generally 1-2 orders smaller than variance term; reducing f without increasing p saturates NMSE; for p ≃ 0.8 no gain in accuracy for ln f < 10⁻⁶ on short sequences, but for longer sequences low-energy states found late and large fluctuations remained at ln f ≈ 10⁻⁶, so ln f_final = 10⁻⁸, p = 0.8 chosen; this needs 3-4 orders of magnitude fewer MC trials than ln f_final = 10⁻¹⁰, p = 0.999. Authors kept standard f ← √f rule (no 1/t algorithm).
- p. 7: WL resampling for structural observables: continue with ln f ≤ 10⁻⁷ from final g(E), stop when all visited histogram bins have ≈ 10⁸/10⁹ hits; bootstrap with 200 resamples for errors (Fig. 4 caption).
- p. 7: 64mers (ten 3D sequences, Ref. 55): same putative ground-state energies as nPERM [58] and FRESS; WLS timings few seconds to ~1 h; nPERM few seconds to 8 h (2 GHz PC); no FRESS timings. Details not shown.
- p. 9: found all currently known putative ground-state energies for the benchmark sequences, including E = −58 for 3D103 (also obtained by constraint-based CPSP, Ref. 29, which is not a blind search). History for 3D103: Lattman et al. 1994 E = −44 (hydrophobic zipper), contact interactions −49, PERM variants −54/−55/−56, FRESS −57, WLS −58 (Ref. 30). The probability of an E = −58 conformation is "more than 20 orders of magnitude smaller" than for E = −44 (could not estimate relative DOS between ground and first excited state).
- p. 8-9: transition temperatures: 2D100a Tc ≈ 0.49 (55% H), 2D100b Tc ≈ 0.48 (56% H), 2D85 Tc ≈ 0.65 (69.4% H); no sequence studied has a ground-state energy gap; 3D124 and 3D136 show sharp drops in DOS (several orders of magnitude) between lowest states; 3D136 folding transition near kT/ε ≈ 0.35.
- p. 11-13: N = 500 chains, 50% H/50% P, random vs protein-like (protein-like constructed by marking the 50% of monomers farthest from the centre of mass of compact homopolymer conformations as P, Ref. 68); E_min ≈ −400 for both (T→0 energies −403 random, −405 protein-like, Fig. 7); DOS steeper at low energies for protein-like; collapse Tc ≈ 1.2 (protein-like) vs ≈ 0.9 (random); typical energies at Tc −240 (protein-like) vs −193 (random). DOS for N = 500 obtained by splitting into two unequal energy windows (lower covering 10-15% of range, overlap e.g. 25 levels), > 95% of energy range accurately estimated "within a couple of CPU days"; ground state for these lengths "out of reach for any method".
- p. 13 (conclusion): DOS of HP 36mer adsorbing on a weakly attracting surface in ≈ 5.5 h (t_DOS), 1-2 orders faster than Ref. 69, vs ≈ 28 h for the WL + configurational-bias scheme of Ref. 8; method also used for interacting SAWs with N ≫ 1000 (Refs 30, 71).

## Methods / algorithms
1. (p. 2) State space: SAW conformations on Z² or Z³, HP sequence fixed; energy = −ε × number of non-bonded H–H nearest-neighbour contacts. Target: g(E) over the full energy range.
2. (p. 2-3) Driver: Wang-Landau random walk in energy space with acceptance Eq. (2), g and H updated after every trial; f reduced by √ when histogram flat (H ≥ p⟨H⟩ for all visited E); f_final ln = 10⁻⁸ and p = 0.8 for production.
3. (p. 3) Self-adaptive energy range: no pre-run; new energy found ⇒ marked visited, g set to g_min, H reset; flatness checked on visited energies at long intervals.
4. (p. 3-4) Move set: pull moves (Ref. 40; reversible, ergodic; internal and chain-terminal; the chain-terminal "hook" pull is not allowed, non-reversible), bond-rebridging (Ref. 41 types 1 and 2 internal, plus chain-terminal; leave monomer positions unchanged; monomer types relabelled afterwards to keep the HP sequence), pivot moves (Ref. 37, 39).
5. (p. 4-5) Detailed balance without enumeration: moves chosen with constant, conformation-independent probability; invalid ⇒ rejected (g, H of old state updated). Pull: pick at random from a precomputed list of size max n_pull (Eq. 3) for the fully elongated chain. Rebridging: random integer i ∈ [0, N]; i = 0 or 1 ⇒ terminal move at one or other end, else internal type 1 or 2 starting at the bond between monomers i−1 and i. Pivot: random non-identity element of the d!·2^d lattice symmetry matrices applied to the shorter part of the chain at a random pivot point.
6. (p. 5) Fixed move fractions 75% / 23% / 2% (pull / rebridging / pivot).
7. (p. 5) Implementation: coordinate vectors + bit table (L = N + 2, periodic); incremental energy; MT19937 RNG.
8. (p. 6) Thermodynamics from g(E) (Eqs. 5-6); structural observables via WL resampling (Eqs. 7-8).
9. (p. 7) Benchmarking protocol: record E_min, t_hit and t_DOS; 20 independent WL runs, interquartile mean; compare to nPERMis and FRESS timings (different CPUs, so only indicative).
10. (p. 12) Long chains: split DOS into two unequal energy windows with overlap.
- Compute: hardware 2.8 GHz AMD Opteron 2439 (WLS); nPERMis on 167 MHz Sun ULTRA I (Table I) / 667 MHz DEC ALPHA 21264 (Table II; 2D85 on 167 MHz Sun ULTRA I); FRESS on 1.4 GHz PC.
- Verification: exact DOS comparison on 20mer (2D) and 14mer (3D) (NMSE, Fig. 2); agreement of putative ground states with literature values; independent RNG (RANLUX) cross-check (p. 5).

## Stated limitations, open problems, future work
- p. 6: no generic optimal rule for reducing f has been found; 1/t algorithm needs a system-dependent parameter and an improper choice may not converge.
- p. 8: comparisons of timings limited by different CPUs/implementations.
- p. 9: NA / unsampled ground state for 3D103 −58, 3D124 −75 and 3D136 −83 (WLS needs ≥ 10 days or ≈ 90-104 h with prior DOS).
- p. 10: "it still remains a challenge to 'design' a (computationally affordable) structural observable which reflects the cooperative rearrangements" ; τ's interpretation as folding funnel/barrier signature is a conjecture, "left for further research".
- p. 12: for N = 500 finding the ground state is "out of reach for any method"; p. 13: systematic analysis of random vs protein-like over a large ensemble of sequences left as an interesting topic.
- p. 13-14: parallelisation (energy windows, multiple walkers, replica-exchange-like conformation swapping) would likely give substantial speed-ups.
