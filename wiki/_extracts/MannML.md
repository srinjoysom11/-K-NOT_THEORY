## Abstract
Lattice knots have been studied in recent years, especially in Z³ and with respect to how many edges are required to form a knot. Knots formed from cubes have also been investigated for their ability to tessellate space. In this article, we demonstrate that there is a relationship between the minimum number of edges required to form a lattice knot and the minimum number of cubes required to form the same kind of knot. We further investigate the relationship in the face-centered cubic lattice.

## Notation and conventions
- (p. 1) Z³ = {(x,y,z) | x,y,z ∈ Z}; steps = unit segments with endpoints in Z³; a Z³-lattice knot of type K = simple closed polygonal cycle of steps forming a knot of type K; **Z³-minimal step number** = least number of steps. Footnote: the term is "sometimes called the lattice number … or the minimal edge number"; authors propose "minimal step number" to avoid confusion over "edge".
- (p. 3) m_K = Z³-minimal step number of K; M_K = minimal cube number of K.
- (p. 2) *Lattice cubes*: unit cubes whose centres lie in the cubic lattice, faces parallel to coordinate planes. A **cube knot** of type K = connected cycle of lattice cubes {C_1,…,C_n} such that (1) consecutive C_i meet face-to-face; (2) centres of consecutive C_i joined by steps form a Z³-lattice knot of type K (the *core knot*); (3) distinct bars (straight sequences of cubes) intersect iff they are consecutive. Volume = number of cubes. Well-formed: consecutive bars may intersect only at a common corner cube (p. 3).
- (p. 3–4) Definition 1: for each integer i, the cubes of D whose centres have z-coordinate i form connected components; those of volume > 1 are the *z_i-components* ("slab components"); x_i-, y_i-components analogous. Components of volume 1 are vertical bars passing through the slab. Types (p. 5): Type I both ends connect upward; Type II both ends connect downward; Type III one up, one down.
- (p. 8) Voronoi cell of p ∈ L: V(p) = {x ∈ R^n : |p−x| ≤ |q−x| for all q ∈ L}; L generated by independent v_1,v_2,v_3.
- (p. 9) FCC lattice "generated by the vectors (1,0,0), (0,1,0), and (1/2, 1/2, 1/√2)" — ⚠ as printed; this is a different normalisation from the FCC basis in JvRR12comp (±1,±1,0)…; its Voronoi cell is the rhombic dodecahedron. L-steps are "segments whose endpoints are adjacent points of L" (p. 1; adjacency not further defined).

## Numbered statements
- **Theorem 1 (p. 4).** For any knot type K, the minimal cube number is twice the Z³-minimal step number. That is, M_K = 2·m_K. *Proved here.*
  Proof idea (paper's own outline, p. 4): scale a minimal lattice knot by 2 and put cubes at all vertices to get M_K ≤ 2·m_K (trefoil: 48 cubes, Fig. 4); for ≥, arrange any cube knot D without changing knot type or increasing volume so that the end (corner) cubes of every slab component have centres on the even integer lattice; scale the core by 1/2 to get a lattice knot of length ≤ ½·vol(D); if vol(D) < 2m_K this is shorter than m_K, impossible. The arrangement moves all odd slab components up (or all down): moving a Type I component up decreases volume by 2 (down: +2), Type II up increases by 2 (down: −2), Type III up or down no change (p. 8); with N_I, N_II odd components of types I, II, the total change is −2N_I+2N_II (up) or 2N_I−2N_II (down), one of which is ≤ 0; all odd components are moved simultaneously to avoid abutting; repeat in x and y.
- **Open Question 1 (p. 9).** For a given lattice L and nontrivial knot type K, let m_K be the minimum number of L-steps required to form a knot of type K, and let M_K be the minimum number of Voronoï cells of L needed to form a knot of the same type. For every lattice L, does there exist a real number C_L such that M_K = C_L·m_K?
- **Conjecture 1 (p. 11).** For the face centered cubic lattice, M_K = 2.5·m_K.
- **Open Question 2 (p. 11).** What is the minimal step number of the figure eight knot in Z³ and in the face-centered cubic lattice? (Z³: conjectured 30 [2]; FCC: "We conjecture that the minimal step number is 20".)
- **Open Question 3 (p. 11).** For lattices corresponding to the hexagonal prism, rhombic dodecahedron, hexarhombic dodecahedron, and truncated octahedron, what is the minimal step number of the trefoil knot?

## Key equations and quantitative claims outside numbered statements
- p. 1: Diao [1] showed the trefoil has Z³-minimal step number 24 (rigorous, quoted); figure-eight Z³-minimal step number 30 "is conjectured in [2]".
- p. 1–2: trefoil conjectured to have minimal step number 16 in the FCC lattice [3] (Fig. 2: 16-step FCC trefoil, from [4]).
- p. 4: m_trefoil = 24 ⇒ M_trefoil = 48 cubes (Fig. 4; rigorous consequence of Thm 1 + Diao).
- p. 9: Fedorov [8]: only five combinatorial types of Voronoï cells (parallelohedra): cube, hexagonal prism, rhombic dodecahedron, hexarhombic dodecahedron, truncated octahedron.
- p. 9: authors "conjecture that a trefoil knot formed from rhombic dodecahedra requires a minimum of 40 cells" (Fig. 9) and a figure-eight "a minimum of 50" (Fig. 10). FCC figure-eight lattice knots of step size 20 have been found (conjectured minimal).
- p. 9 ⚠ "Thus it seems that C_L = 2 in the face centered cubic lattice" — but 40/16 = 50/20 = 2.5 and Conjecture 1 (p. 11) states 2.5; the two statements are inconsistent as printed.
- p. 9: scaling the 16-step FCC trefoil by 2 and using it as the core of a rhombic dodecahedron knot (32 cells) is not well-formed: it self-intersects at vertices of nonconsecutive bars (Fig. 11); so the cores of the 40-cell knot are not minimal knots scaled by 1/2.

## Methods / algorithms
Proof strategy of Theorem 1 (pp. 4–8):
1. Upper bound: scale a minimal Z³ knot by 2; place cubes at all 2m_K vertices of the scaled knot; well-formed since nonconsecutive straight pieces were ≥1 step apart.
2. Lower bound: classify slab components (Types I–III); move all odd slab components of one coordinate direction up simultaneously (or all down), chosen so that volume change ≤ 0.
3. Repeat in x and y: result D′ with the same knot type, volume ≤ vol(D), corner cubes of every slab component centred on the even integer lattice (no self-intersection at any step).
4. Scale the core of D′ by 1/2: lattice knot of length ≤ ½ vol(D′); hence vol(D) ≥ 2m_K.
