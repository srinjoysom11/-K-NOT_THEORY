# JL11 — Prime knots whose arc index is smaller than the crossing number
- **Authors:** Gyo Taek Jin, Hwa Jeong Lee
- **Venue / year / identifiers:** arXiv:1106.2723v1 [math.GT], 13 Jun 2011 (MSC 57M25, 57M27)
- **File:** papers/3_analytic_bounds/Jin-Lee_2011_Arc_index_smaller_than_crossing_number_1106.2723.pdf   **Pages:** 30

## Abstract
It is known that the arc index of alternating knots is the minimal crossing number plus two and the arc index of prime nonalternating knots is less than or equal to the minimal crossing number. We study some cases when the arc index is strictly less than the minimal crossing number. We also give minimal grid diagrams of some prime nonalternating knots with 13 crossings and 14 crossings whose arc index is the minimal crossing number minus one.

## Notation and conventions
- Arc presentation: link embedded in a book of finitely many half planes in R³, each half plane meeting the link in a single arc; arc index α(L) = minimal number of half planes (p. 1). Grid diagram: union of finitely many vertical strings and the same number of horizontal strings, vertical string over horizontal at every crossing; the minimal number of vertical strings among grid diagrams equals the arc index (p. 1).
- c(L) = minimal crossing number; F_L(v,z) Kauffman polynomial; spr_v(F_L) = v-spread = highest minus lowest degree in v (p. 1).
- Wheel diagram; knot-spoke diagram; spoke; cut-point; edge contraction D_e (pp. 3–4). Filtered tree T_0 ⊂ T_1 ⊂ … ⊂ T_k, closure, good/bad edge, cutting arc, good filtered tree, doubly good edge (nonalternating edge, p. 5–7). (B1), (B2), (B3)-extensions (p. 7).
- (n,1)-tangle: alternating tangle diagram of n+1 crossings with projection as in Fig. 13(a); (n,1)-nonalternating diagram: decomposes into two alternating tangles one of which is an (n,1)-tangle; n-tangle and n-nonalternating via Fig. 13(b); a 1-nonalternating diagram is an almost alternating diagram (p. 9).
- Knot names such as 13n0563, 14n7534 are Knotscape/Dowker–Thistlethwaite names (nonalternating, crossing number 13 or 14) (p. 16). ⚠ Not a ropelength paper: no ropelength convention.

## Numbered statements
- **Proposition 1.1 (Cromwell) (p. 2).** Every link admits an arc presentation. *Quoted.*
- **Theorem 1.2 (Cromwell) (p. 2).** If L_1 and L_2 are nontrivial links, then α(L_1 ♯ L_2) = α(L_1) + α(L_2) − 2. *Quoted.*
- **Theorem 1.3 (Bae–Park) (p. 2).** If L is a nonsplit link, then α(L) ≤ c(L) + 2. *Quoted.*
- **Theorem 1.4 (Morton–Beltrami) (p. 2).** For every link L, α(L) ≥ spr_v(F_L(v,z)) + 2. In particular, if L is an alternating link, then α(L) ≥ c(L) + 2. *Quoted.*
- **Theorem 1.5 (Jin–Park) (p. 2).** A prime link L is nonalternating if and only if α(L) ≤ c(L). *Quoted.*
- **Corollary 1.6 (p. 2).** A prime nonalternating link L satisfies spr_v(F_L(v,z)) + 2 ≤ α(L) ≤ c(L). *Follows from 1.4 and 1.5.*
- **Lemma 2.1 (Bae–Park) (p. 4).** Let D be a prime knot-spoke diagram without cut-points with at least two multi-valent vertices. Then there are at least two non-loop non-spoke edges e and f, incident to v_0, such that D_e and D_f have no cut-points. *Quoted.*
- **Lemma 2.2 (Jin–Park) (p. 5).** Let D be a prime cut-point free knot-spoke diagram and e an edge incident to v_0 and to another 4-valent vertex v_1 such that D_e has a cut-point. Then there exists a simple closed curve S_e with (1) D_e ∩ S_e = v_0; (2) S_e separates ē and ē'; (3) S_e separates D_e into two knot-spoke diagrams D̄ and D̄' containing ē and ē' respectively; D̄' is prime and cut-point free and there is a sequence of non-spoke edges e_1,…,e_k of D not in D̄' such that D_{e_1…e_k} is identical with D̄' on non-spoke edges on one side of S_e and has only spokes on the other side. *Quoted.*
- **Theorem 3.1 (Theorem 1.3 rephrased) (p. 6).** A prime link diagram D admits a good filtered spanning tree and therefore one can obtain an arc presentation with c(D)+2 arcs.
- **Theorem 3.2 (Theorem 1.5 rephrased) (p. 7).** A prime nonalternating diagram D of a link has a good filtered spanning tree which has at least two doubly good edges so that D has an arc presentation with c(D) arcs.
- **Proposition 3.3 (p. 7).** Let T_0 ⊂ … ⊂ T_m be a non-spanning good filtered tree in a prime diagram D; e an edge with T_m ∩ e a single vertex. If T_0 ⊂ … ⊂ T_m ⊂ (T_m ∪ e) is not a good filtered tree, then T_m ∪ e has a bad edge or a sufficiently small neighborhood of T_m ∪ e has disconnected exterior in D.
- **Lemma 4.1 (Lemma 2.1 rephrased) (p. 8).** Let D be a prime knot diagram with c(D) crossings and T_0 ⊂ … ⊂ T_m a non-spanning good filtered tree. Then there are two edges e and f in D∖T_m such that T_m ∪ e and T_m ∪ f are good extensions of T_m.
- **Lemma 4.2 (Lemma 2.2 rephrased) (p. 8).** (Statement about a simple closed curve S for non-good extensions when m < c(D) − 2; see p. 8.) Remark: if T_m ∪ e is a (B3)-extension, e_1,…,e_k can be chosen with e_k = e.
- **Lemma 4.3 (p. 8).** Innermost cutting arcs Γ_p, Γ_q of T_i, T_j (i<j) can be isotoped to have disjoint interiors. (Proof omitted in the paper.)
- **Lemma 4.4 (p. 8).** Let T_0 ⊂ … ⊂ T_m be a non-spanning good filtered tree and sp, pq, qr three consecutive edges along ∂F with ∂F ∩ T_m = ∅ and pq ∩ T_m = ∅ (as printed). Then there is a sequence of good extensions T_{m+1} ⊂ … ⊂ T_{m+k} such that the closure contains all edges of ∂F except sp, pq, qr.
- **Lemma 4.5 (p. 9).** Let T_m be a non-spanning good filtered tree, sp, pq, qr consecutive edges of ∂F with pq nonalternating and ∂F∖{sp,pq,qr} ⊂ T̄_m. Then pq becomes a doubly good edge of T̄_{m+2} if (1) T_{m+1} = T_m ∪ sp and (2) T_{m+2} = T_{m+1} ∪ rq are good extensions.
- **Corollary 4.6 (p. 9).** Under Lemma 4.5's setting, if (1) T_{m+1} = T_m ∪ sp is a (B3)-extension with a tree T' having a good extension T' ∪ sp containing T_{m+1}, and (2) T' ∪ sp ∪ rq is a good extension of T' ∪ sp, then pq is a doubly good edge of T' ∪ sp ∪ rq.
- **Lemma 4.7 (p. 9).** Let F be a face of a minimal crossing diagram D of a prime knot with a nonalternating edge pq on ∂F, vertices v_0,…,v_{n−2} = q, v_{n−1} = p (n ≥ 3). Then there is a good filtered tree T_0 ⊂ … ⊂ T_m with T_0 = v_0, v_i ∈ T_m (i = 0..n−3), T_{m+1} a good extension along v_{n−3}q. Furthermore if the extension T_{m+2} along v_0p is not (B3) then pq is a doubly good edge of T̄_{m+2}.
- **Corollary 4.8 (p. 9).** If the hypothesis of Lemma 4.7 holds and v_0 and p are two vertices of a bigonal face adjacent to F, then pq is a doubly good edge of T̄_{m+2}.
- **Theorem 4.9 (p. 10).** Let n ≥ 2 and D a prime (n,1)-nonalternating minimal crossing knot diagram having a nonalternating triangular face F_3, with faces F_1, F_2, F_3, F, edges e_1, e_2 and a vertex q labeled as in Fig. 14. Then α(D) < c(D) if D satisfies: (1) F satisfies e_1 ∪ e_2 ⊂ ∂F and F ∩ (F_1 ∪ F_2) = ∅; (2) there are two vertices v ∈ ∂F ∪ {q}, w ∈ ∂F_2 and a string a_{vw} of D joining v and w such that no edge of ∂F_1 ∪ ∂F_3 is contained in a_{vw}. *Proved (n = 2 case in detail, §5.1; "easily adapted for n > 2").*
- **Theorem 4.10 (p. 10).** Let n ≥ 2 and D a prime, n-nonalternating, minimal crossing knot diagram having a nonalternating triangular face F_3 (Fig. 15). Then α(D) < c(D) if: (1) F satisfies e_1 ∪ e_2 ⊂ ∂F and F ∩ (F_1 ∪ F_2) = ∅; (2) there are two vertices v ∈ ∂F, w ∈ ∂F_2∖{q} and a string a_{vw} of D joining v and w such that no edge of ∂F_1 ∪ ∂F_2 ∪ ∂F_3 is contained in a_{vw}; (3) ∂F_2 consists of at least n + 3 edges. *Proof details skipped (§5.2).*
- **Theorem 4.11 (p. 10).** Let D be a prime, almost alternating, minimal crossing knot diagram having a nonalternating triangular face F_3 (Fig. 16); F the union of two faces containing e in the intersection of their boundaries. Then α(D) < c(D) if: (1) F ∩ (F_1 ∪ F_2) = {q}; (2) there are two vertices v ∈ ∂F, w ∈ ∂F_2 and a string a_{vw} joining v and w such that no edge of ∂F_1 ∪ ∂F_3 is contained in a_{vw}. *Proof details skipped (§5.3).*

## Key equations and quantitative claims outside numbered statements
- p. 2: Theorems 1.3 and 1.4 imply α(L) = c(L) + 2 for nonsplit alternating links.
- p. 2: Table 1 counts (see Tables). Prime nonalternating knots with spr_v + 2 = c have α = c.
- p. 13–15 (figure captions, results verified by the paper's grid diagrams): Thm 4.9 examples α(8n3) = 7, α(12n475) = 10, α(12n725) = 10; non-examples α(8n2) = 8, α(12n699) = 12, α(12n305) = 12. Thm 4.10 examples α(12n810) = 11, α(11n110) = 10, α(12n847) = 11; non-examples α(12n777) = 12, α(12n389) = 12, α(12n767) = 12. (Captions p. 13–16; notation as printed, e.g. "12n475".)
- p. 16 (quoted): Nutt identified all knots up to arc index 9; Beltrami arc index for prime knots up to 10 crossings; Jin et al. all prime knots up to arc index 10; Ng arc index for prime knots up to 11 crossings; Jin–Park prime knots up to arc index 11.
- p. 16: the 13-crossing knots in the lists do not appear in [7] (all prime knots up to arc index 11); grid diagrams with 12 vertical arcs found, so α = 12 = c − 1 for them (the lower bound via Kauffman v-spread, implicit in "arc index 12").
- p. 28: the 14-crossing knots in the lists have Kauffman v-spread equal to 11, hence arc index at least 13; grid diagrams with 13 vertical arcs found, so α = 13 = c − 1.
- p. 28 (list size): 7.1 lists 364 knot entries over 7 classes (see CSV; counts from extraction: (2,1): 293, (3,1): 5, 2-nonalt.: 46, 3-nonalt.: 12, 4-nonalt.: 2, 5-nonalt.: 1, almost alternating: 5), 7.2 lists 15 entries. ⚠ these counts are my tallies of the printed names, not stated in the paper.

## Methods / algorithms
1. (p. 3–5) Bae–Park knot-spoke diagrams: contract edges of a prime knot diagram until only spokes and one non-simple loop remain (properties (5)–(7)); folding the last loop gives c(D)+2 spokes = arcs.
2. (p. 5–7) Filtered spanning tree formulation: good filtered tree has no bad edges / cutting arcs; each doubly good edge (nonalternating) lets a nonalternating triangular region be contracted and saves one arc.
3. (p. 8–9) Lemmas 4.4–4.7: constructing good extensions around a face with a nonalternating edge.
4. (p. 10–13) Main theorems: apply a type-3 Reidemeister move over F_3, then build a good filtered tree in which three nonalternating edges become doubly good in succession (Steps 1–3 for Thm 4.9, n = 2).
5. (p. 16) Search: Dowker–Thistlethwaite codes in Knotscape for 13- and 14-crossing knots that are (n,1)-nonalternating, n-nonalternating or almost alternating; test the theorem conditions; draw minimal grid diagrams.
