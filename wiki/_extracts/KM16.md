## Abstract
Ropelength and embedding thickness are related measures of geometric complexity of classical knots and links in Euclidean space. In their recent work, Freedman and Krushkal posed a question regarding lower bounds for embedding thickness of n-component links in terms of the Milnor linking numbers. The main goal of the current paper is to provide such estimates, and thus generalizing the known linking number bound. In the process, we collect several facts about finite type invariants and ropelength/crossing number of knots. We give examples of families of knots, where such estimates behave better than the well known knot–genus estimate.

## Notation and conventions
- (p. 1) Links are C^1 embeddings L = (L_1,…,L_n): S^1 ⊔…⊔ S^1 → R^3. "its ropelength rop(L) is the ratio rop(L) = ℓ(L)/r(L) of length ℓ(L), which is a sum of lengths of individual components of L, to reach or thickness r(L), i.e. the largest radius of the tube embedded as a normal neighborhood of L." So ropelength = length / **radius** (not diameter): a unit-thickness knot has r = 1 and rop = ℓ.
- (p. 1) Rop(L) = inf_{L'∈[L]} rop(L') (infimum achieved, minimiser C^{1,1}, quoted from [9]).
- (p. 1) Embedding thickness τ(L) = reach r(L) when L ⊂ unit ball B_1; 𝔗(L) = sup over isotopy class.
- (p. 3) Cr(K) = minimal crossing number; cr(K) / cr(D) = number of crossings of a diagram; acr(K) = average crossing number (average over all projections, Eq. (4.2)); PCr(L) = pairwise crossing number, Eq. (4.14): PCr(L) = min_{D_L} Σ_{i≠j} cr_{i,j}(D_L). (Note footnote 2: PCr(L) ≤ Cr(L).)
- (p. 9) ov_{i,j}(D_L) = number of times L_i overpasses L_j in D_L; cr_{i,j} = ov_{i,j} + ov_{j,i}; aov_{i,j}(L) = (1/4π)∫_{S^2} ov_{i,j}(v) dv; acr_{i,j} = 2 aov_{i,j} (Eq. 4.2). (⚠ as printed (4.1) says Σ over i≠j in PCr but cr_{i,j}=cr_{j,i}; sums over i<j elsewhere, so factor 2 conventions differ between (4.14) and (4.13)/(5.3); not resolved in paper.)
- (p. 2) Milnor μ̄-invariants μ̄_{I;j}(L), I=(i_1,…,i_k); with distinct indices they are link-homotopy invariants ("Milnor linking numbers" / higher linking numbers). μ̄_{I;j}(L) ≡ μ_{I;j}(L) mod Δ_μ(I;j), Δ_μ(I;j) = gcd(Γ_μ(I;j)) (1.8). [μ̄] := min(μ̄, d − μ̄) for d>0 and |μ̄| for d=0, d=Δ_μ(I;j) (1.9). "top" Milnor linking numbers: μ̄_{23…n;1} and its (n−2)! permutations (p. 13 fn. 9).
- (p. 2) Lk(L_i, L) = "maximal total linking number between L_i and the other components"; Ac = asymptotic crossing number [19]; g(L_i,L) = minimal genus of surfaces in complement of the other components.
- (p. 5–6) Gauss diagram G_K: arrows from under- to over-crossing, signs; arrow polynomial ⟨A, G⟩ = Σ_φ sign(φ) over base-point-preserving embeddings. Z_{n;1} = arrow (tree) polynomial for μ̄_{23…n;1} of Komendarczyk–Michaelides [26]/Kravchenko–Polyak [28].

## Numbered statements
**Question A (p. 2) — open; from Freedman–Krushkal [18, p. 1424].** Let L be an n-component link which is Brunnian (almost trivial in the sense of Milnor [34]). Let M be the maximum value among Milnor's μ̄-invariants with distinct indices, i.e. among |μ̄_{I;j}(L)|. Is there a bound τ(L) ≤ c_n M^{−1/n} (1.7), for some constant c_n > 0, independent of the link L? Is there a bound on the crossing number Cr(L) in terms of M?

**Theorem A (p. 2 and again p. 12 with "of unit thickness") — proved here.** Let L be an n-component link, n ≥ 2, and μ̄(L) one of its top Milnor linking numbers. Then rop(L)^{4/3} ≥ ∛n · ( [μ̄(L)]^{1/(n−1)} ) and Cr(L) ≥ (1/3)(n−1) [μ̄(L)]^{1/(n−1)}. (1.10, 5.3). [p. 12 version: ℓ(L) ≥ ∜n·(([μ̄(L)])^{1/(n−1)})^{3/4}, same content since ℓ = rop at unit thickness; ∜n·x^{3/4} = (∛n·x)^{3/4}.]
Consequence stated p. 2 (using (1.4)): τ(L) ≤ (11/∜n)^{1/3} M^{−1/(4(n−1))}.
Proof idea: bound |⟨Z_{n;1}, G_L⟩| by a product of pairwise crossing numbers (5.4)–(5.5) via Lemma J, relate ⟨Z_{n;1},G_L⟩ = μ̄ + k d, integrate over directions of projection (4π α_n [μ̄]^{1/(n−1)} ≤ Σ_{i<j}∫cr_{i,j}), apply Lemma F + Jensen (5.7), and Stirling for α_n ≥ (n−1)/3 (5.8).

**Question B (p. 3) — open.** Can we find estimates for ropelength of knots/links, in terms of their finite type invariants?

**Remark D (p. 4) — question.** Can we find a family of knots with constant c_{2n}-coefficient (or any finite type invariant, see Remark L), but arbitrarily large genus? (Note: there exist knots with c_2 = 0 and nonzero genus, such as 8_2, for which (1.19) still gives a nontrivial bound.)

**Theorem B (p. 4, restated p. 6) — proved here (Polyak–Viro-type argument).** Given a knot K, Cr(K) ≥ ((2n)! |c_{2n}(K)|)^{1/2n} ≥ (2n/3) |c_{2n}(K)|^{1/2n}. (1.17, 3.3)
Proof idea: ⟨C_{2n}, G_K⟩ counts embeddings of arrow diagrams, each embedding covers a distinct 2n-subset of the cr(K) crossings, so |c_{2n}| ≤ C(cr(K), 2n) ≤ cr(K)^{2n}/(2n)!; the second inequality uses Stirling m! ≥ √(2π) m^{m+1/2} e^{−m} and e^{−1}... ≥ 1/3 (3.4).

**Corollary C (p. 4) — proved here.** For a unit thickness knot K, ℓ(K) ≥ ½ ( d_0 + √( d_0² + (128 n π /3) |c_{2n}(K)|^{1/2n} ) ), d_0 = 10 − 6(π+√2) ≈ 17.334 (1.18). (Obtained by combining (1.17) with Diao's bound (1.14).)

**Lemma F (p. 9) — proved here (based on [8], [5]; rearrangement idea from [8]).** For a unit thickness link L and any 2-component sublink (L_i, L_j), with ℓ_i=ℓ(L_i), ℓ_j=ℓ(L_j): min(ℓ_i ℓ_j^{1/3}, ℓ_j ℓ_i^{1/3}) ≥ 3 aov_{i,j}(L), and ℓ_i ℓ_j ≥ 16π aov_{i,j}(L). (4.3)
Proof idea: Gauss-map / coarea formula: aov_{i,j} = (1/4π)∫∫ |det(L_i'(s),L_j'(t),L_i−L_j)|/‖L_i−L_j‖³ ds dt ≤ (1/4π)∫∫ ‖L_i−L_j‖^{−2}; bound the "illumination" I_i(p) of L_i from a point p=L_j(t) by comparing tube volume πs(z) ≤ (4/3)π(z³−1) in shells and the monotone rearrangement inequality: I_i(p) ≤ 3(4/3)^{2/3} ℓ_i^{1/3}. The second estimate follows from ‖L_i−L_j‖² ≥ 4 (distance ≥ 2).

**Corollary H (p. 11) — proved here.** Let L be an n-component link (n≥2). Then rop(L)^{4/3} ≥ (6 n^{1/3}/(n−1)) Σ_{i<j} |Lk(L_i,L_j)| and rop(L)² ≥ (32π n²/(n²−1)) Σ_{i<j} |Lk(L_i,L_j)|. (4.13)

**Corollary I (p. 11) — proved here.** Let L be an n-component link (n≥2), PCr(L) its pairwise crossing number. Then rop(L)^{4/3} ≥ (3 n^{1/3}/(n−1)) PCr(L) and rop(L)² ≥ (16π n²/(n²−1)) PCr(L). (4.15)

**Lemma J (p. 12) — proved here.** For nonnegative a_1,…,a_N and k ≥ 2: Σ_{1≤i_1<…<i_k≤N} a_{i_1}⋯a_{i_k} ≤ (1/N^k) C(N,k) (Σ_{i=1}^N a_i)^k. (5.1) Proof idea: the ratio is maximal when all a_i are equal.

**Remarks (non-numbered but stated claims).**
- Remark E (p. 9): cyclic symmetry of μ̄: μ̄_{ρ(2)…ρ(n);ρ(1)}(L) = μ̄_{23…n;1}(L) for cyclic ρ ([35, Eq. (21)]).
- Remark G (p. 10): definition of monotone (symmetric decreasing) rearrangement f* and the inequality ∫f ≤ ∫f*.
- Remark K (p. 12): estimate (5.5) is valid for any arrow polynomial with arrows based on different components and no parallel arrows on a given component.
- Remark L (p. 14): extension to other finite-type invariants; for the third Conway coefficient of a 2-component link: |⟨C_3,G_L⟩| ≤ (cr_{1,2}(D_L)+cr(D_{L_1}))³, hence |c_3(L)|^{1/3} ≤ acr_{1,2}(L)+acr(L_1); for unit thickness, acr_{1,2}+acr(L_1) ≤ (1/3)ℓ_1^{1/3}ℓ_2 + (1/3)ℓ_2^{1/3}ℓ_1 + (4/11)ℓ_1^{4/3}, and ≤ ℓ_1²/16π + ℓ_1ℓ_2/8π. "for some constants α, β > 0": ℓ(L)² ≥ A|c_3(L)|^{1/3}, ℓ(L)^{4/3} ≥ B|c_3(L)|^{1/3}. In general "we may expect constants α_{m,n}, β_{m,n}": ℓ(L)² ≥ α_{m,n}|V_n(L)|^{1/n}, ℓ(L)^{4/3} ≥ β_{m,n}|V_n(L)|^{1/n} (conjectural / expected, not proved). ## Key equations and quantitative claims outside numbered statements
All of these are quoted from the literature unless marked.
- (1.4, p. 1) For L ⊂ B_1: tube volume πℓτ² and tube ⊂ ball of radius 2 ⇒ rop(L) = πℓτ²/(πτ³) ≤ (4/3)π2³/(πτ³) ⇒ τ(L) ≤ (11/rop(L))^{1/3}. (⚠ as printed (11/rop)^{1/3}; (4/3)·8/π... derivation printed gives 4/3π2³/(πτ³)=32/(3τ³), whose cube root coefficient is (32/3)^{1/3}≈2.2; the printed constant 11 ≈ 32/3 rounded up; consistent with "≤".)
- (1.5, p. 2) [9, Thm 11]: rop(L_i) ≥ 2π + 2π √(Lk(L_i, L)).
- (1.6, p. 2) [9]: rop(L_i) ≥ 2π + 2π √(Ac(L_i, L)) and rop(L_i) ≥ 2π + 2π √(2g(L_i, L) − 1), using Ac ≥ 2g − 1 (Freedman–He [19]). Estimate (1.6) subsumes (1.5) since Ac ≥ Lk.
- (1.11, p. 3) Buck–Simon: ℓ(K) ≥ ((4π/11) Cr(K))^{3/4}, ℓ(K) ≥ 4√(π Cr(K)) (unit thickness); first better for large Cr, second sharper for low-crossing knots (e.g. trefoil).
- (1.12, p. 3) from average crossing number: ℓ(K)^{4/3} ≥ (4π/11) acr(K), ℓ(K)² ≥ 16π acr(K).
- (1.13, p. 3) this paper, for n-component links (n≥2): ℓ(L) ≥ (1/√(n−1)) ((3/2) PCr(L))^{3/4}, ℓ(L) ≥ (n√(16π)/√(n²−1)) (PCr(L))^{1/2}. ⚠ The first inequality as printed in (1.13) does not match Corollary I (4.15), which gives ℓ ≥ (3 n^{1/3} PCr/(n−1))^{3/4}; the paper does not reconcile these (for n=2 the (1.13) form is the weaker one).
- (1.14, p. 3) Diao [12]: ℓ(K) ≥ ½(d_0 + √(d_0² + 64π Cr(K))), d_0 = 10 − 6(π+√2) ≈ 17.334 (footnote: 16π Cr(K) ≤ ℓ(K)(ℓ(K) − 17.334)).
- (1.15) c_2 vs crossing number: ¼Cr(Cr−1) + 1/24 ≥ |c_2(K)| (Lin–Wang [30]); (1/8)Cr² ≥ |c_2(K)| (Polyak–Viro [38]). (1.16): ¼Cr(Cr−1)(Cr−2) ≥ |V_3(K)| (Willerton [43]). Bar-Natan [3]: type-n invariant V has |V(K)| = O(Cr(K)^n).
- (1.19, p. 4) [9] for knots: ℓ(K) ≥ 2π + 2π√(Ac(K)) and ℓ(K) ≥ 2π + 2π√(2g(K) − 1).
- Pretzel examples (p. 4): g(P(a,b,c)) = 1, c_2(P(a,b,c)) = (1/4)(ab+ac+bc+1) for odd a,b,c of the same sign ([20]); so lower bound (1.18) with n=1 grows without bound as a,b,c → ∞ while the genus bound (1.19) stays constant. Also D(m,k) = P(m, ε, …, ε) (|k| copies of ε=k/|k|), genus 1, c_2(D(m,k)) = mk/4.
- Example (p. 6): c_2(5_2) = 2 from Gauss diagram (all crossings positive; two embeddings {a,d},{c,d}).
- (5.13, p. 14) Borromean rings (n=3, N=3, α̃_3=3, d=0 so μ̄_{23;1}=1): rop(L) ≥ (5·∛3·√|μ̄_{23;1}(L)|)^{3/4} and rop(L) ≥ 6√(6π) · |μ̄_{23;1}(L)|^{1/4}. "Since 6√(6π) ≈ 26.049, the second estimate is better for Borromean rings (μ_{23;1} = 1) and improves the linking number bound of (1.5): 6π ≈ 18.85, but fails short of the genus bound (1.6): 12π ≈ 37.7." "Numerical simulations suggest that the ropelength of Borromean rings ≈ 58.05, [9, 7]." (⚠ with α̃_3 = 3 and (5.12), the first constant would be 3·∛3·3/2 = 4.5∛3, not 5∛3 as printed; the printed 5∛3 could not be reproduced from (5.12). Second constant is consistent: 64π·3·(9/8) = 216π ⇒ √(216π) = 6√(6π).)
- (5.12, p. 13) Borromean-type improved constant: α̃_n = ( (1/(n N^{n−1})) C(N, n−1) )^{−1/(n−1)}, N = C(n,2); rop^{4/3} ≥ (3∛n α̃_n/(n−1)) [μ̄_{n;1}]^{1/(n−1)} and rop² ≥ 4³ π α̃_n (n²/(n²−1)) [μ̄]^{1/(n−1)} (second printed with garbled grouping; as in the page image).
- (5.6) α_n = ( (1/N^{n−1}) C(N,n−1) )^{−1/(n−1)}; (5.8) α_n ≥ ((n−1)!)^{1/(n−1)} ≥ (n−1)/3 for n≥2.
- (5.7) rop^{4/3} ≥ (3 n^{1/3} α_n/(n−1)) [μ̄_{n;1}]^{1/(n−1)}.

## Methods / algorithms
Pure-math / inequality chain. Steps as in the paper:
1. (p. 3–4) Reduce to unit thickness (rop scale-invariant); combine crossing-number lower bounds on ℓ (Buck–Simon, Diao) with finite-type bounds on Cr.
2. (p. 5–7) Theorem B: write c_{2n} as signed count of ascending one-component arrow diagrams C_{2n} in Gauss diagram (3.2, from [11]); count embeddings injectively into 2n-subsets of crossings.
3. (p. 7–8) Recursive definition of Z_{n;1} (stacking elementary trees e, ē on components, discarding duplicates, sign (−1)^q with q = #right-pointing arrows; ē not allowed on the first component, the "trunk"); formula μ̄_{n;1}(L) ≡ ⟨Z_{n;1}, G_L⟩ mod Δ_μ(n;1) (3.5); permutation identity (3.7)–(3.8) gives all distinct-index μ̄.
4. (p. 9–11) Lemma F via Gauss map F_{i,j}: S^1×S^1→S^2 and coarea formula; Corollaries H, I via sum over pairs and Jensen's inequality.
5. (p. 12–13) Proof of Theorem A: (5.4) product bound, (5.5) with Lemma J, (5.6) integrate over S^2, Lemma F, Jensen, Stirling.
6. (p. 13) Improvement for Borromean-type (d=0) using cyclic symmetry (5.10)–(5.12).

## Stated limitations, open problems, future work
- Question A and Question B (pp. 2, 3) are posed; the paper answers A for general (not necessarily Brunnian) n-component links via Theorem A (as an upper bound on τ), but not with the Freedman–Krushkal exponent −1/n (obtains −1/(4(n−1))).
- "A relation between Ac(L_i, L) and the higher linking numbers of Milnor, [34, 35] is unknown and appears difficult." (p. 2)
- Remark D (reverse question) is open (p. 4).
- p. 14: for Borromean rings the new bound 26.049 "fails short of the genus bound (1.6): 12π ≈ 37.7" and of the numerical ropelength ≈ 58.05.
- p. 14: general constants α_{m,n}, β_{m,n} for arbitrary finite type invariants are only "expected".
