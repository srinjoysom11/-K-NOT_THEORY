## Abstract
The ropelength of a knot is the minimum contour length of a tube of unit radius that traces out the knot in three dimensional space without self-overlap, colloquially the minimum amount of rope needed to tie a given knot. Theoretical upper and lower bounds have been established for the asymptotic relationship between crossing number and ropelength and stronger bounds have been conjectured, but numerical bounds have only been calculated exhaustively for knots and links with up to 11 crossings, which are not sufficiently complex to test these conjectures. Existing ropelength measurements also have not established the complexity required to reach asymptotic scaling with crossing number. Here, we investigate the ropelength of knots and links beyond the range of tested crossing numbers, past both the 11-crossing limit as well as the 16-crossing limit of the standard knot catalog. We investigate torus knots up to 1023 crossings, establishing a stronger upper bound for T(p,2) knots and links and demonstrating power-law scaling in T(p,p+1) below the proven limit. We investigate satellite knots up to 42 crossings to determine the effect of a systematic crossing-increasing operation on ropelength, finding that a satellite knot typically has thrice the ropelength of its companion. We find that ropelength is well described by a model of repeated Hopf links in which each component adopts a minimal convex hull around the cross-section of the others, and derive formulae that heuristically predict the crossing-ropelength relationship of knots and links without free parameters.

## Notation and conventions
- **Ropelength convention: LENGTH / RADIUS** — "the minimum contour length of a tube of unit radius" (abstract, p. 1); e.g. ideal 1-fold Hopf link = two circles of radius 2 with ropelength 8π (p. 4); unit cross-sectional radius, curves must stay ≥ 2 apart (p. 6). Consistent with CKS01/CFM02, and a factor 2 from DDS04 (length/diameter). The paper notes "a radius-diameter convention change" (factor 2) when importing Kallrath–Frey convex-hull perimeters L_KF (p. 5–6).
- L = ropelength, C = crossing number (p. 1). Unknot has 0 crossings and ropelength 2π in the intercept remark (p. 3).
- N-fold Hopf link: two sets of N circles, every circle of one set Hopf-linked with every circle of the other, none within a set; 2N components, 2N² crossings; 1-fold = Hopf link; 2-fold = four-loop closed Hopf chain 8^4_1 = L8a21 (p. 4).
- T(p,q) torus knot/link; crossing number = (larger parameter)·(smaller − 1); T(p,p+1) has p² − 1 crossings (p. 6–7).
- σ = π/√12 ≈ 0.91 = hexagonal close-packing fraction (p. 5). L_KF = convex hull perimeter around N unit disks from Kallrath & Frey [38] (p. 5).
- Launching: building basic double (1-tangle closure, +1 crossing), Whitehead double (2-tangle clasp, +2 crossings), linked double (∞-tangle) from knot coordinates (p. 8–10).
- Effective exponent of launching = ln(ropelength ratio)/ln(crossing ratio) (p. 10).
- Numerical tool: Ridgerunner (Cantarella), constrained gradient optimization (p. 3–4); ropelength values are upper bounds, "not definitive".

## Key equations and quantitative claims outside numbered statements
**Quoted / literature bounds (p. 3):**
- (1) L ≤ a_u C ln⁵ C (Diao–Ernst–Por–Ziegler [13], proven; constant a_u not stated there). Authors note a_u ≥ 8π/ln⁵2 ≈ 78.5 "in order to bound the Hopf link". ⚠ 8π/ln⁵2 ≈ 157; the value 78.5 corresponds to 4π/ln⁵ 2 (Hopf link: C = 2, L = 8π ⇒ a_u ≥ 8π/(2 ln⁵ 2)). Printed as "8π/ln⁵2 ≈ 78.5" (p. 3 image). Slightly smaller if the unknot (0 crossings, ropelength 2π) is included as intercept.
- Largest previously annealed knot 11a327 is exceeded by the (1) bound by a factor 691 (using 78.5); if conjectured L ≤ a_u C ln² C [Diao et al. 28, numerical/conjectural] the prefactor must exceed 26.2 and 11a327 is exceeded by factor 16.7 (2.4 if unknot included) (p. 3).
- (2) L ≥ a_l C^{3/4} (Buck [29], proven). Proven prefactor a_l = (4π/11)^{3/4} ≈ 1.105. Upper bound on the prefactor constrained by the 10_124 knot (= T(5,3)): a_l ≲ 12.64, 11.4× the proven bound. Authors "argue" a_l ≈ 8.65 (heuristic) (p. 3).
- (3) L ≥ ½(17.334 + √(17.334² + 64πC)) (Diao [21], proven), "stronger than the 3/4 bound for knots up to 1850 crossings" (p. 3). (Equivalent to 16πC ≤ L(L − 17.334) as quoted in CKS01 p. 25.)
- p. 2–3: prior ropelength calculations: Pieranski SONO up to 9 crossings; Millett–Rawdon stochastic crankshaft up to 9; Ashton et al. constrained gradient: prime knots ≤ 10 crossings, prime links ≤ 9, connected sums ≤ 13; Gilbert (Knot Atlas, SONO): all prime knots and links ≤ 11. Four exhaustive calculations with three algorithms agree within 0.1%. Trefoil bound: 32.7433864 (2004 [23]) → 32.7429345 ([24]).
- p. 2–3 (numerical fit, prior data): ensemble power-law exponent of ropelength vs crossing number: single-component knots 0.81 ± 0.03; multi-component links 0.81 ± 0.05; two-component compound knots 0.86 ± 0.01 (Fig. 1b fit exponent 0.81). First two torus knots 3_1 and 5_1 grow with exponent 0.71 (p. 3). Alternating knots longer than non-alternating beyond 8 crossings (p. 3).
- p. 3: Dai–Doyle [26] best-fit exponent between crossing number and contour length 0.826 (open knots up to 50 crossings, not true ropelength); Diao et al. [28] lattice embeddings up to 3174 crossings, fit to linear-times-log-squared (numerical).
- p. 3 (quoted): ropelength exponent range 3/4 to 1 for asymptotic power-law (Buck); families with any exponent in that range exist [14, 15]; Hopf-link chains give linear growth, asymptotically large tori of Hopf-linked circles 3/4 power.

**Hopf-link model (heuristic / explicit constructions) (pp. 4–6):**
- 1-fold Hopf link: ropelength 8π (two radius-2 circles).
- 2-fold Hopf link: each component a stadium (two radius-2 semicircles joined by segments of length 2), length 4 + 4π per component; ropelength 16(π+1) ≈ 66.26, "reached within 1% in Ridgerunner"; a one-parameter family of ideal configurations (rotation about long axis).
- Stadium family for N-fold Hopf link: linear part of each component 2(N − 1), circumference 2(2(N − 1) + 2π); total ropelength with 2N components 8(N² + (π − 1)N). (The paper prints the intermediate expression as "N(2(N−1)+2π)" — ⚠ should be 4N(2(N−1)+2π); the closed form 8(N²+(π−1)N) is consistent with 2N components, and gives 16(π+1) at N = 2.) Not minimal for N > 2.
- (4) L_s(C) = 8( C/2 + (π − 1)√(C/2) ). Large-C slope 4, "about half that of other proven linear relations [15, 36]".
- 4-fold Hopf link configuration (rounded squares bound four stadia): ropelength 16(2π + 5) ≈ 180.5 (Fig. 2 caption; upper bound, minimal-hull construction).
- (5) L_t(N) = (2N)·2π√(N/σ) = (4π/√σ) N^{3/2} (packed Hopf tori, neglecting that inner components are shorter). N = σ πR²/π ⇒ R = √(N/σ).
- (6) L_t(C) = (4π/√σ)(C/2)^{3/4} = (4/√π)(6C)^{3/4} ≈ 8.65 C^{0.75} (hexagonal packing). ⚠ Numerically (4π/√σ)(1/2)^{3/4} = 7.85, not 8.65; 8.65 = (4/√π)·6^{3/4} equals the middle expression divided by σ. Printed as given (p. 5 image). Heuristic lower-prefactor estimate; "not asserted minimal"; twisting of bundles not considered.
- (7) L_h = 4N(L_KF + π) = 4√(C/2)(L_KF + π): ropelength of N-fold Hopf link with each component a minimal convex hull (Kallrath–Frey perimeter L_KF, solved to ≈ 20 disks, sub-optimal up to 90 disks) around N unit disks; component length 2L_KF + 2π. Power-law fit to the convex-hull model between 1 and 20 components (2 to 800 crossings): exponent 0.73 ± 0.02 (numerical/model).
- p. 4: Cantarella et al. theorem [34] (quoted): length of a component in an ideal link ≥ that of the minimal convex hull around identical disks.

**Torus T(p,2) (pp. 6–8):**
- (8)–(10) harmonic parametrization x = (r cos qθ + R) cos pθ, y = (r cos qθ + R) sin pθ, z = −r sin qθ.
- (11) Huh–Kim–Oh [36] (quoted, rigorous upper bound for (6k+1,2) torus knots): L_HT(k) = 45.8237k + 28.4223 ⇒ L_HT(C) = 7.63C + 20.78 (C = 6k + 1; arithmetic: 7.637C + 20.785). The slope 7.63 compared with 2π + 2 ≈ 8.28 for chain-link Hopf sums; the 7_1 knot has ropelength ≈ 82% of L_HT.
- (12) Authors' T(2k,2) link construction (circle with a helix of k twists wound at radius 2 around it): non-overlap 2π(R−2)/k ≥ 2 ⇒ R ≥ k/π + 2 (equality at minimum). (13) arc length of one twist L_t = ∫₀^{2π} [4 + (1/π + (2/k)(1 − cos θ))²]^{1/2} dθ ≈ 2π√(4 + 1/π²) ≈ 12.72 (large-p limit, helix of pitch length and radius 2). (14) L_l = 2π(2 + k/π) + k L_t. (15) With C = 2k: L_l(C) ≈ (1 + π√(4 + 1/π²)) C + 4π ≈ 7.36C + 12.56 — an upper bound for T(2k,2) links (rigorous construction, integral approximated); "within 1% of the numerical solution after 34 crossings"; "stronger than that established by Huh et al. for (6k+1,2) torus knots". ⚠ Eq. 15 is derived for links; the T(25,2) knot weakly violates it (attributed to coarse-graining, 200 vertices) (p. 7).
- Numerical: T(p,2) knots and links annealed with 200 total vertices (100 per component for links) up to 25 crossings; linear trend; all satisfy the Huh bound; no break at C = 6k + 1; all links satisfy Eq. 14 (p. 7). Annealed T(16,2): initial contour length 132.5 → 127.8 after minimisation (Fig. 3 caption).
- T(63,2) needs 123 vertices vs T(8,9) (also 63 crossings) 34 vertices (p. 7).

**Torus T(p,p+1) (pp. 7–8):**
- Annealed up to 1023 crossings (Fig. 5). Tracks the Hopf-stadium curve (Eq. 4) to 15 crossings then approaches the packed-torus 3/4 limit slowly (estimated ≈ 200,000 crossings to reach it). Best-fit exponent 0.71 ± 0.01 (numerical), "seemingly violating the 0.75 lower bound"; authors state it is a finite-size effect that persists beyond 1000 crossings and not a mathematical violation.
- Convex-hull Hopf model (Eq. 7) agrees "remarkably" with annealed T(p,p+1) data with no free parameters; both fitted exponents below 3/4 and consistent. Implication claimed: large knots show true 3/4 scaling only when convex hulls of disks near √C obey asymptotic 1/2 scaling.

**Satellites (pp. 8–11):**
- Basic double of the trefoil has 4×3+1 = 13 crossings. Launching increases crossing number conjecturally by 4× plus the tangle-closure crossings (basic +1, Whitehead +2). Lattice-based launching: lattice knot with V vertices → basic double 2V+1 vertices, Whitehead 2V+3 (p. 10).
- Trefoil with 24 lattice vertices exceeds best value by 18%; 8_1 with 50 vertices only 1.6% above (p. 10). Whitehead double of trefoil: 51 vertices → 107.32; 102 vertices → 104.57; 204 vertices → 104.41 (≈3% lower than coarse-grained) (p. 10).
- Table I (p. 10): mean ropelength ratio satellite/companion: link 2.98 ± 0.01, basic 3.01 ± 0.01, Whitehead 3.19 ± 0.01 (numerical, knots up to 8 crossings, i.e., satellites up to 42 crossings). Observed ratios within the expected range 2.63 to 4 (2-fold Hopf link has ropelength ≈ 2.63× the Hopf link with 4× fewer crossings; a doubled-and-translated lattice knot gives 4×). Whitehead double of 8_19: factor 2.94 (effective exponent 0.746); linked double of 8_19: 2.73 (0.72) (smallest ratios in non-alternating torus knots).
- Conclusion (p. 11, preliminary): quadrupling crossings triples ropelength ⇒ exponent ln 3/ln 4 ≈ 0.79, similar to prime-knot fit; "further research is required".
- Stadium model (Eq. 4) runs slightly below ensemble-averaged satellite ropelength (p. 10). Linked repetitions of 4_1 and 8_19 up to 200 crossings: well-described by the stadium equation; repeated 8_19 more compact (convex hull model) (p. 10).

## Methods / algorithms
1. (p. 3–4) Software: Ridgerunner (Cantarella) on AWS virtual machine; minimises polygon length over vertex coordinates under a no-overlap constraint using projected-gradient steps on a polyhedral cone of admissible perturbations; terminates when the projected gradient is a small fraction (typically 0.01 or 0.001) of the original gradient.
2. (p. 4) Two stages: run with equilateralization force until residual gradient 0.1; use result as input to a second run without equilateralization, terminating residual 0.001.
3. (p. 4) Knot coordinates typically generated in MATLAB; KnotPlot used for visualisation and preconditioning ("dynamic" mode).
4. (p. 4) Topology check by Vologodskii et al. algorithm: Alexander polynomial evaluated at −1 from coordinates, before and after annealing (does not exclude knots with the same Alexander polynomial). Unknotting observed only when vertex count near stick number, and in rare satellite cases; then repeat with more vertices.
5. (p. 6–7) T(p,2): 200 total vertices; T(p,p+1): fewer vertices per crossing needed.
6. (p. 10, 13) Satellite launching from Scharein et al. minimal cubic-lattice embeddings: two copies translated by (0.5, 0.5, 0.5), concatenated with 1–3 extra points. Appendix: points A (first of knot 1), B (first of copy), C (last of knot 1), D (last of copy); basic double joins A–D and B–C with additional point H above midpoint along AB × AD; Whitehead double with E at midpoint of AB (contour C E D), F at midpoint of AC shifted along AB × AC by one lattice unit, G at midpoint of BD shifted along −AB × BD; concatenation F, first knot, E, second knot reversed, G.

## Stated limitations, open problems, future work
- p. 8: authors "are not claiming to have discovered mathematically impossible results" — the apparent violation of the 3/4 bound is a finite-size effect; number of vertices may be argued insufficient but more vertices would lower the exponent further (p. 8).
- p. 7: a true minimum for T(2k,2) may be derivable by relaxing the circle constraint; additional twisting may be a path to minimisation.
- p. 10: satellite ropelengths, companion ropelengths, and satellite crossing number (above 19 crossings) are unproven upper bounds; coarse-graining bias because more complex knots have higher lattice number.
- p. 11: "further research is required" to determine whether quadrupling crossings triples ropelength holds generally; a geometric derivation of the minimum length of a torus-bound Whitehead unknot may explain the factor 3.
- p. 11 future work: 360 satellite knots up to 19 crossings [43]; torus knots beyond T(p,2), T(p,p+1); annealing lattice-generated random knots as statistical ensembles; "is the ropelength minimizing configuration of a knot the same as that which minimizes the volume of its convex hull?" (open question).
- p. 5–6: exact N-fold Hopf configuration for intermediate N unknown; Spaghetti-O conjecture (torus packing fraction slightly smaller) mentioned.
