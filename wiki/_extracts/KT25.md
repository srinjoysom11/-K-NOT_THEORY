## Abstract
An alternating torus knot or link may be constructed from a repeating double helix after connecting its two ends. A structure with additional helices may be closed to form a non-alternating torus knot or link. Previous work has optimized the dimensions and pitch of double helices to derive upper bounds on the ropelength of alternating torus knots, but non-alternating knots have not been studied extensively and are known to be tighter. Here, we examine concentric helices as units of non-alternating torus knots and discuss considerations for minimizing their contour length. By optimizing both the geometry and combinatorics of the helices, we find efficient configurations for systems with between 3 and 39 helices. Using insights from those cases, we develop an efficient construction for larger systems and show that concentric helices distributed between many shells have an optimized ropelength of approximately 7.83 Q^{3/2} where Q is the total number of helices or the minor index of the torus knot, and the prefactor is exact and a 75% reduction from previous work. Links formed by extending these helices and bending them into a T(3Q, Q) torus link have a ropelength that is approximately 12 times the three-quarter power of the crossing number. These results reduce the ratio between the upper and lower bounds of the ropelength of non-alternating torus knots from 29 to between 1.4 and 3.8.

## Notation and conventions
- **Ropelength convention: LENGTH / RADIUS.** "the minimum contour length that a knot can have while respecting the no-overlap constraint of a tube of unit radius" (p. 1). For helices "representing a tube of unit radius ... no two helices can be within distance 2 of each other" (p. 2). Same convention as KM21; factor 2 from DDS04.
- N-helix (Eq. 1): helix i: x = R cos(θ + 2πi/N), y = R sin(θ + 2πi/N), z = Hθ/(2π), θ ∈ [0,2π]. Crossings per twist: N(N−1). Helix arc length per twist L_h = √(H² + (2πR)²) (Eq. 2). Height H, radius R.
- Q = total number of helices including a central rod = smaller index of T(P,Q) torus knot/link (p. 2). "Q-multihelix": central rod plus N = Q−1 helices spread over T shells; arrangement notation 1-A-B-C…, e.g. 1-4-5-2 (rod, shells of 4, 5, 2) (p. 5). M_i = helices in shell i.
- C = crossing number; crossings per helical twist ≈ Q² (p. 7); T(pQ, Q) with p units has crossing number pQ(Q−1) (p. 10); T(3Q,Q) has C ≈ 3Q² (p. 16).
- Figures of merit: ropelength per crossing (L/C) per twist; L/C^{3/4}; "prefactor" (p. 7, 10).
- σ = π/√12 hexagonal packing fraction (p. 16).
- p = number of helix units around the torus (lower case) vs P = pQ (p. 10). R_M = Hp/(2π) major radius; closure replaces p by p+1 (p. 10).
- Hull lower bounds use minimal convex hulls around unit-diameter disks by convention (Kallrath et al.), with offset 2π for the surrounding component: L_lower = Q(2 L_H,3(Q−1) + 2π) (p. 16).

## Key equations and quantitative claims outside numbered statements
**Quoted background (p. 1–2):**
- Best proven lower bounds on alternating torus knot/link ropelength per crossing: 1/28 for links [Diao 2], 1/56 for knots [Diao 16] (rigorous, quoted). Upper bounds per crossing for alternating T(P,2): Bohr–Olsen 17 per twist = 8.5 per crossing [12]; Huh–Oh–Kim 7.63 [13]; Klotz–Maldonado 7.36 [14]; Kim et al. 7.32 [15] (current best, construction).
- Non-alternating: proven sublinear scaling at least C^{3/4} [Buck–Simon 3]: L ≥ κ C^{3/4}, proven κ ≥ 1.105 [23] ((4π/11)^{3/4}); Diao–Ernst lattice construction [5] achieves C^{3/4} with coefficient between 20 and 32 ("not precisely defined"; "upper bound for lattice torus knots of 32" p. 11); Cantarella et al. [17] used helical geometry for the same exponent. Smallest published L/C^{3/4} of a specific knot 10.76 [14] (p. 10). For every crossing number at which a torus knot exists and all ropelengths measured (3, 5, 7, 8n, 9a, 10n, 11a), the tightest knot is a torus knot (p. 1).
- p. 17: Appendix correction of earlier work (KM21): the hexagonally packed hull argument for Hopf linkings of two sets of untwisted bundles gives limiting ropelength 8√(π/2)·6^{1/4} ≈ 15.7 C^{3/4}, not 8.65 C^{3/4} [14]; "the result of both a factor-of-two error and a minor algebraic error". Also: in the derivation of the proven prefactor (4π/11)^{3/4} ≈ 1.10, the "11" was an approximation replaceable by 10.67, giving 1.13 (p. 17).

**Highly concentric helices (Section V, pp. 7–9):**
- Incremental construction (k ≥ 5 helices added per shell): example 1-5-10-15-20: shells at radii 2.29, 4.50, 6.75, 9.00, height 56.57, ropelength per twist 3001.7. Q = (k/2)T(T−1)+1 ≈ (k/2)T², T = √(2Q/k).
- (13) L_α/C = 1.72k²T³/((k/2)T²)² = 6.88/T = 6.88√(2/(kQ))... ⚠ printed as "6.88 √(2/(kQ)) ≥ 10.89/√Q" (image p. 7). Result L/C ≳ 10.89/√Q (heuristic/construction).

(17) L_β/C < 2πT/Q + 4/T (upper bound, proven by Appendix Eqs 31–33 for N_s > π/2, i > 1). (18) minimised at T = √(2Q/π): L_{β0}/C < 4√(2π/Q) ≈ 10.03/√Q. If optimization skipped and ideal values used, only ~1% longer. Example: 720 helices about a central rod minimised at 20 shells of 36 helices each (Fig. 4a).

**Closure (Section VI, pp. 9–11):**
- Torus closure of p units gives T(pQ, Q) with Q components; major radius R_M = Hp/(2π); to avoid overlap increase R_M by the outer minor radius, equivalent to replacing p by (p+1) (p. 10). Connectors for a single-component knot add at least 4Q length and (Q−1) essential crossings; give T(pQ+Q−1, Q) (p. 11); closure length negligible for large knots; no algorithm for constructing the linkages that respects no-overlap (p. 11–12).
- (25) L/C^{3/4} ≈ ((p+1)/p) 10.89 p Q^{3/2}/(pQ²)^{3/4} = 10.89 p^{1/4} ((p+1)/p), minimised at p = 3, giving prefactor 19.11 (factor 1.76 above 10.89). Overestimate since outer helices are underconstrained.
- Optimised 1-5-7-6 helix closed into T(57,19): L/C^{3/4} = 11.54 (2093 = 11.54 × 1026^{3/4}). Lower values for optimised T(3Q,Q) in Fig. 5c are below the ratio for any numerically tightened knot below 13 crossings, and are surpassed by numerical tightenings of T(Q+1,Q) with ≥ 224 crossings [14]. Table III's "best prefactor" column gives values 11.5–13.9 for Q = 2…39.
- Conjectural lower bound: each of Q components of T(3Q,Q) has linking number 3 with the others ⇒ ≥ minimal convex hull around 3(Q−1) disks: L/C^{3/4} → √(8π) ≈ 5.01 (large Q, circular hull, hexagonal packing; Appendix Eqs 36–38; ≈ 5.55 for Q = 2). Upper bound 19.11 is a factor 3.8 above 5.01 (reduced from 29 = 32/1.1 between Diao–Ernst's lattice 32 and 1.1) (p. 11). Using best known hulls the lower bounds are between 7 and 9.5 for the range of Fig. 5c; lowest ratio of constructed upper bound to hull-based lower bound 1.41 for T(93,31). T(2163, 721) link (1,557,360 crossings) from the optimised 721-multihelix has ratio 12.06 ("most complex specific knot discussed in the literature to date") (p. 11). Best-fit exponent for T(3Q,Q) links ropelength vs crossing number 0.73 (compare 0.71 for T(Q+1,Q) in KM21).
- Comparison with Diao–Ernst [5]: for any specific Q configuration with coefficient ≈ 12; established upper bound 19.11; "60 to 75% reduction of the linear upper bound, and a 40 to 60% reduction in the three-quarters bound" (p. 11).
- p. 16: T(4,2) Solomon link: hull-based lower bound 33.1 (conjectural), 18% below best computed upper bound [7] and 31% stronger than the best previously published lower bound [4]. (These lower bounds are conjectural because many minimal hulls above N = 6 are only conjectured minimal.)
- Appendix (Eqs 26–28, p. 13): X(R,N) = R/N − (1/N)/sin(π/N) ≈ R/N − 1/π, Y(H,N) = H/N − 2; fits Y(X) = 0.0643 X^{−1.8612}/(0.1932 X^{−1.8612} + 1)^{0.5556}, X(Y) = 0.1301 Y^{−1.3239}/(0.4165 Y^{−1.3239} + 1)^{0.5896}; passes through ideal values at (0.132, 0.828) (numerical fits, conjectural). Cylindrical N-helix radius cannot be below 1/sin(π/N) ≈ N/π; height not below 2N (p. 13).
- Appendix D (Eqs 36–38, p. 16): σπR² = 3(Q−1)π ⇒ R ≈ √(3Q/σ); L_lower = Q·2πR = 2π√(3/σ) Q^{3/2}; with C ≈ 3Q²: L_lower/C^{3/4} = 2π√(3/σ)Q^{3/2}/(3Q²)^{3/4} = √(8π) ≈ 5.01.
- Ridgerunner checks (p. 11): T(48,16) link as three windings of a 1-5-10 concentric helix with 199 vertices per helix: initial contour length rescaled from 2139.7 to 2141.6 (non-overlap check; with 100 vertices per helix rescaled to 2146); plateau at 1480.55 (30% below the non-optimised initial configuration); more efficient 1-5-7-3 reached plateau 1572 (local minima).

## Methods / algorithms
1. (p. 2) Parametrise N-helix (Eq. 1); no-overlap condition = minimal distance between neighbouring helices ≥ 2, giving transcendental equation (3) for φ_N (Huh et al. [19]); solve for φ_N numerically, then R_o, H_o (4).
2. (p. 4) Replace one helix by a straight rod in the centre (Caduceus-type), allowing smaller radius/height.
3. (p. 5–6) Multihelix: shells at radii R, R+2, R+4, …; height set by the most constrained shell; for each of 2^{N−1} compositions minimise L over (H, R) with constraints 2 ≤ R ≤ 4, H ≥ 2 max M_i, min distance between phase-shifted neighbours in each shell ≥ 2 (500-vertex discrete helices; MATLAB fmincon interior-point).
4. (p. 7–9) Large-Q constructions: incremental (k per shell), equal-per-shell (optimising number of shells and inner radius via fit H̃), infilling (inner shells with 4i helices), reverse-Jenga (move helices from outermost shells to the innermost shell that can take them without overlap, checked with discrete helices).
5. (p. 9–11) Closure: wrap into torus (major radius R_M = H(p+1)/(2π)) or two parallel multihelices with connectors; T(pQ,Q) link or T(pQ+Q−1, Q) knot.
6. (p. 11) Validation with Ridgerunner for overlaps.

## Stated limitations, open problems, future work
- p. 12: truly minimal multihelix may be found for Q > 39 if an exact expression for H̃(R) were available; relaxing rigid features: allow central rod to twist slightly (Kim et al.; small reduction, same asymptotics); non-circular cross-sectional layout (circle not hull-minimal until N in the hundreds); higher Fourier modes / cycloidal helices (a few percent tighter in experiments, not pursued); twisting two parallel multihelices as a superhelix. "It is unknown whether the best form of such a superhelix would be a symmetric double helix or something closer to a rod of Asclepius."
- p. 11–12: no algorithm for closing two parallel multihelices into a single-component knot respecting no-overlap; "if the connections require a series of arcs whose required radius increases with Q, the scaling of the connector's length may not be negligible".
- p. 13: fit functions H̃, R̃ are conjectural with limited accuracy; configurations from them should be checked.
- p. 16: lower bounds from minimal hulls are conjectural for N > 6.
- p. 11: Ridgerunner annealing of these links falls into local minima; escape would require symmetry breaking.
