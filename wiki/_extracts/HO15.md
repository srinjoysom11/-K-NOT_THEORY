## Abstract
The lattice stick number of a knot type is defined to be the minimal number of straight line segments required to construct a polygon presentation of the knot type in the cubic lattice. In this paper, we mathematically prove that the trefoil knot 3_1 and the figure-8 knot 4_1 are the only knot types of lattice stick number less than 15, which verifies the result from previous numerical estimations on this quantity.

## Notation and conventions
- Cubic lattice Z³ = (R×Z×Z) ∪ (Z×R×Z) ∪ (Z×Z×R) (p. 1). Lattice knot = polygon in Z³ (p. 1).
- Stick = straight line segment of a polygon (may span several lattice units). Lattice stick number s_L(K) = minimal number of sticks of a lattice knot of type K (p. 2). s_L(trivial knot) = 4 (p. 2). Not a ropelength / length-per-thickness quantity; no ropelength convention applies.
- Minimum step number (unit edges) is mentioned as a different quantity (p. 1).
- Total curvature of a lattice knot of type K is (π/2)·s_L(K), since adjacent sticks meet at angle π/2 (p. 2).
- Polygon = polygonal knot in Z³; equivalent = ambient isotopic; reducible if an equivalent polygon has fewer sticks, otherwise irreducible (p. 2). |P| = number of sticks; |P|_x, |P|_y, |P|_z = numbers of x-, y-, z-sticks (p. 2). (Printed "|P|_x(resp. y, z-sticks)" with a typo |P|_x for the z-count.)
- x-level k = plane with x-coordinate k; levels labeled 1..n; levels 1 and n are boundary levels (p. 2–3).
- Properly leveled: each x- (resp. y-, z-) level contains exactly two endpoints of x- (resp. y-, z-) sticks; then P has |P|_x x-levels etc. (p. 3).

## Numbered statements
- **Theorem 1 (p. 2).** s_L(K) = 14 if and only if K is 4_1. *Proved here (reduced to Lemma 4 using results of [8]).*
- **Corollary 2 (p. 2).** s_L(K) ≥ 15 for every non-trivial knot K except for 3_1 and 4_1. *Follows from Theorem 1 and the result of [8] (s_L(3_1) = 12 and s_L(K) ≥ 14 for any other nontrivial K).*
- **Lemma 3 [8] (p. 3, quoted from Huh–Oh 2005, "modified").**
  (1) For a polygon P, there is a properly leveled polygon P' equivalent to P with |P'| = |P|.
  (2) Let P be a properly leveled polygon. If two x-sticks of P have their endpoints on the same x-levels, then the knot type of P must be trivial (similarly for y or z-sticks).
  (3) Let P be a properly leveled irreducible non-trivial polygon. Then each boundary level contains only one stick or two sticks which are connected. Furthermore, if a stick of P has one endpoint on a boundary level, then this stick has length at least two.
- **Lemma 4 (p. 3).** Let P be a properly leveled irreducible non-trivial polygon. If (|P|_x, |P|_y, |P|_z) = (5, 5, 4), then P is 4_1. *Proved in §3 (p. 3–7).*
- **Lemma 5 (p. 3).** Let p: R³ → R² be the projection into the xy-plane. Then the intersection between the two simple arcs p(P_2°) and p(P_3°) should be transverse. *Proved here.* Proof idea: a non-transverse point-contact would force connecting arcs needing more than one z-stick; a segment overlap is excluded by irreducibility (stick count reducible by 1) or by adjacency of x-sticks.
- **Lemma 6 (p. 4).** p(P_2° ∪ P_3°) has at least two transverse double points. *Proved here.* Proof idea: with no double points, pull P_3 into plane Z_2 and delete z_23, giving fewer sticks; with exactly one, the diagram of P has ≤ 3 crossings, so P is trivial or 3_1, contradicting irreducibility.
- **Lemma 7 (p. 5).** p(P_2° ∪ P_3°) does not contain any of two types of pairs of subarcs as in Figure 4. *Proved here* (case analysis, Figs 5–6): such a pair Q has six or eight sticks; each case forces P reducible or an impossible placement of z_14.

## Key equations and quantitative claims outside numbered statements
- p. 1 (quoted, rigorous): Diao [1]: minimum step number of any nontrivial knot is ≥ 24, and only 3_1 can be realized with 24 steps. Minimum step number of 4_1 and 5_1 are 30 and 34 [15] (Scharein et al.; "it was reported").
- p. 2 (quoted): Milnor: total curvature of any nontrivial smooth knot ≥ 4π [12].
- p. 2 (quoted): Janse van Rensburg–Promislow [11]: s_L(K) ≥ 12 for any nontrivial knot (rigorous), plus numerical estimates (simulated annealing) of s_L for various knots; [15] gives other numerical estimates; [8]: s_L(3_1) = 12 and s_L(K) ≥ 14 for any other non-trivial K. From [11, 15] "it can be conjectured that s_L(K) ≥ 15 for any non-trivial knot K except for 3_1 and 4_1" — this paper proves it.
- p. 3: s_L(4_1) ≤ 14 by explicit lattice 4_1 with 14 sticks (Fig. 1 (b), (c)).
- p. 3: for an irreducible nontrivial P with |P| ≤ 14, properly leveled, Lemma 3 gives |P|_x, |P|_y, |P|_z ≥ 4; WLOG |P|_x ≥ |P|_y ≥ |P|_z; so (|P|_x,|P|_y,|P|_z) ∈ {(4,4,4), (5,4,4), (6,4,4), (5,5,4)}. From [8] for |P|_y = |P|_z = 4: (1) (4,4,4) ⇒ P is 3_1; (2) the printed item "(2) (|P|_x, |P|_y, |P|_z) = (5, 4, 4)." has no conclusion printed ⚠ (presumably this case cannot occur / is reducible; not stated); (3) (6,4,4) ⇒ P is 4_1. P has 14 sticks only in cases (6,4,4) and (5,5,4).
- p. 3: with |P|_z = 4, P has four z-levels and four z-sticks z_14, z_13, z_24, z_23; P is the union of eight arcs P_1 → z_13 → P_3 → z_23 → P_2 → z_24 → P_4 → z_14.
- p. 6: Q (pair of subarcs) has either six or eight sticks; |P_2 ∪ P_3| ≤ 8 sticks since P has exactly four z-sticks and P_1, P_4 each consist of one or two sticks.
- p. 7: the final diagram has ≤ 3 crossings (if p(z_14) in B_1 or B_2) or ≤ 4 crossings (if in some A_k); hence P is 4_1 (3_1 would be reducible).

## Methods / algorithms
Pure-math case analysis (no computation).
1. (p. 3) Reduce to properly leveled irreducible polygons; level counts give the four possible (|P|_x,|P|_y,|P|_z) triples; only (5,5,4) is new.
2. (p. 3) Describe P by its eight arcs and z-sticks z_14, z_13, z_24, z_23.
3. (p. 3–4) Lemma 5: arcs p(P_2°), p(P_3°) meet transversally.
4. (p. 4) Lemma 6: ≥ 2 transverse double points; Reidemeister II simplification excludes certain subarc pairs (Fig. 3, p. 4–5).
5. (p. 5–6) Lemma 7: exclude two types of subarc pairs (Fig. 4) via stick counting (Figs 5, 6).
6. (p. 6–7) Describe p(P_2 ∪ P_3) as an n-crossing chain with regions A_1..A_{n−1}, B_1, B_2; place p(z_14) in B_i or A_k; isotope subarcs to obtain a diagram with ≤ 3 or ≤ 4 crossings ⇒ 4_1.
