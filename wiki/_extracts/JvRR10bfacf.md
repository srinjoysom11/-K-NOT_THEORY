## Abstract
In this paper the elementary moves of the BFACF-algorithm [1, 2, 5] for lattice polygons are generalised to elementary moves of BFACF-style algorithms for lattice polygons in the body-centred (BCC) and face-centred (FCC) cubic lattices. We prove that the ergodicity classes of these new elementary moves coincide with the knot types of unrooted polygons in the BCC and FCC lattices and so expand a similar result for the cubic lattice (see reference [16]). Implementations of these algorithms for knotted polygons using the GAS algorithm produce estimates of the minimal length of knotted polygons in the BCC and FCC lattices.

## Notation and conventions
- (p. 5) BCC and FCC are generated by finite sets of basis vectors {c_i} and {e_i}/{d_i}; vertices = integer combinations. Two vertices adjacent iff their difference is a basis vector; two edges adjacent if they share exactly one end-vertex. **Lattice polygon** = list of n adjacent edges u_0u_1,…,u_{n−1}u_0 with all vertices distinct. **Length** = number of edges n ("its geometric length will generally be different … since the edges do not necessarily have length equal to one"). BCC is Eulerian with girth 4, all polygons have even length; FCC is Eulerian with girth 3 (polygons of length ≥ 3).
- (p. 6) BCC basis vectors: c_1=(1,1,1), c_2=(1,1,−1), c_3=(1,−1,1), c_4=(1,−1,−1), c_5=(−1,−1,−1), c_6=(−1,−1,1), c_7=(−1,1,−1), c_8=(−1,1,1); c_5=−c_1, c_6=−c_2, c_7=−c_3, c_8=−c_4; each has geometric length √3. (p. 15) FCC basis: d_1=(1,1,0), d_2=(1,−1,0), d_3=(1,0,1), d_4=(1,0,−1), d_5=(0,1,1), d_6=(0,1,−1), d_7=(−1,−1,0), d_8=(−1,1,0), d_9=(−1,0,−1), d_10=(−1,0,1), d_11=(0,−1,−1), d_12=(0,−1,1); d_{i+6}=−d_i; edge length √2, so a polygon of n edges has geometric length n√2.
- (p. 4) Polygons are **unrooted**; knot type = ambient isotopy class of the oriented PL embedding in R³. Equivalence of polygons = ambient isotopic.
- (p. 5) A *line segment* of a polygon = maximal non-empty run of adjacent edges in one basis direction; *contact*: two non-adjacent vertices v, w with v − w = c_j (BCC) [or a basis vector (FCC)]; contact free polygon has none. Projections: along u transverse to a plane A (non-orthogonal basis {x,y,u}); BCC projects orthogonally to the XY-plane onto a square lattice rotated 45° with edge √2 (p. 7).
- (p. 3, 7) *Plaquette atmosphere*: ways edges can be added/deleted/shuffled around plaquettes adjacent to an edge; a BCC edge has 12 adjacent plaquettes (Fig. 2: nine shown; six planar, six... as printed "six on the right are planar, three on the left non-planar, remaining three mirror images"), an SC edge has ≤ 4, an FCC edge has 4 (Fig. 8).
- (p. 2) BFACF moves of type I (±2 edges, "positive"/"negative" elementary moves) and type II (length-preserving, "neutral").
- (p. 22) GAS parameters: a_+(φ), a_0(φ), a_−(φ) = sizes of the positive/neutral/negative atmospheres; ⟨a_±⟩_N, ⟨a_0⟩_N their means at length N; β_N = ⟨a_−⟩_N/⟨a_+⟩_N (Eq. 2). Sequence φ = (φ_0,…,φ_M); weight W(φ) (Eq. 3); p_n(K) = number of polygons of length n and type K.

## Numbered statements
- **Theorem 1 (p. 4) [quoted, Thm 3.11 of [16] = Janse van Rensburg & Whittington 1991].** The irreducibility classes of the BFACF algorithm, when applied to unrooted polygons [in the simple cubic lattice], are the knot types of the polygons as piecewise linear embeddings in R³.
- **Theorem 2 (p. 12) — proved here.** Let ω be an (unrooted) polygon in the BCC lattice with projection Pω in the XY-, YZ- or XZ-plane. Let L be a line in the AB-plane with formula A ± B = ℓ where (A,B) is one of (X,Y), (Y,Z) or (X,Z). Suppose L intersects the projection Pω and that ℓ is an integer. Suppose also that L lifts to the plane Q_r parallel to the normal of the projection plane. Then ω can be stretched along the plane Q_r in a direction c_i transverse to Q_r by performing the BCC elementary moves in Figures 4 and 5 on ω. *Proof idea:* recursively translate line segments and isolated vertices of ω ∩ P_r (maximal plane) in the c_1 or c_2 direction using the moves of Fig. 6; no self-intersection arises because all parts are translated in parallel.
- **Lemma 3 (p. 12).** By applying the BCC elementary moves in Figures 4 and 5 to an unrooted polygon in the BCC lattice, it can be transformed into a contact free polygon. *Proof:* subdivide (double edges) in the c_i direction in which contacts occur; stretching in direction c_j cannot create new contacts in the c_i direction.
- **Theorem 4 (p. 13).** By applying the classes of BCC elementary moves in Figures 4 and 5 to unrooted polygons in the BCC lattice, any such polygon can be swept into a polygon in the sublattice L with basis {c_1, c_3, c_4, c_5, c_7, c_8}. *Proof idea:* take a contact free polygon, subdivide twice in the c_1, c_5, c_3, c_7 directions; replace runs c_2c_2…c_2 by c_1c_4c_7c_1c_4c_7…, runs c_6… by c_5c_8c_3c_5c_8c_3… (positive non-planar moves), with special treatment of run ends (neutral move c_5c_2→c_4c_7, c_2c_3→c_1c_4; negative move c_5c_2c_3→c_4).
- **Lemma 5 (p. 14).** The BCC elementary moves, restricted to the sublattice L, applied to unrooted polygons in L, have irreducibility classes which coincide with the knot types of the polygons as piecewise linear embeddings in R³. (Corollary of Theorem 1.)
- **Theorem 6 (p. 14).** The irreducibility classes of the BCC elementary moves, applied to unrooted polygons in the BCC lattice, coincide with the knot types of the polygons as piecewise linear embeddings in R³. (Corollary of Thm 4 and Lemma 5.)
- **Lemma 7 (p. 19).** Let ω be any FCC lattice polygon and let Q_r be a lattice plane intersecting ω. Then ω can be stretched, using the FCC elementary move, in Q_r in the d_j direction if d_j is transverse to Q_r.
- **Lemma 8 (p. 20).** By subdividing an FCC polygon, it is possible to transform it into a contact free polygon, using the FCC elementary move. *Proof idea:* subdividing in d_i increases area of every finite face of the projection by ≥ 1 elementary triangle and removes contacts in that direction; subdividing in the six independent FCC directions removes all contacts.
- **Lemma 9 (p. 21).** By using the FCC elementary move, any FCC lattice polygon ω can be swept into a sublattice L of the FCC, where L is ambient isotopic to the simple cubic lattice (L = basis {d_1,d_3,d_5,d_7,d_9,d_11}). *Proof idea:* make ω contact free; replace edges in the even directions by d_2→d_3d_11, d_4→d_1d_11, d_6→d_9d_5, d_8→d_9d_5, d_10→d_7d_5, d_12→d_3d_7 (as printed; ⚠ d_6 and d_8 both map to d_9d_5 in the printed list); re-sweep in {d_1,d_3,d_5} directions if a contact appears.
- **Theorem 10 (p. 21).** The irreducibility classes of the FCC elementary move, applied to unrooted polygons in the FCC, coincide with the knot types of the polygons as piecewise linear embeddings in R³. *Proof idea:* every SC BFACF move in L is a composition of two FCC elementary moves (e.g. d_1 → d_3d_12 → d_3d_1d_9 since d_1 = d_3 + d_12, d_12 = d_1 + d_9; p. 22), then Lemma 9 and Theorem 1.

## Key equations and quantitative claims outside numbered statements
- p. 2, Eq. (1): BFACF samples walks with fixed endpoints from P_β ∝ Σ_n c_n(x,y) e^{βn} weights n e^{βn}, i.e. P_β(n) = n e^{βn} / Σ_n n c_n(x,y)e^{βn}, where c_n(x,y) = number of SAWs of length n from x to y (as printed, garbled: "n eβn / Σ_n n c_n(x,y) eβn").
- BCC elementary moves (pp. 7–9, Figs 4–5): (i) planar positive/negative: replace c_1 by c_2c_1c_6 (and all permutations) (+2 edges) and the reverse (−2); {c_2,c_1,c_6} coplanar, closing gives a square; (ii) non-planar positive/negative: c_1 ↔ c_2c_3c_8 (+2/−2), non-coplanar, closing gives non-planar quadrilateral; (iii) planar neutral: c_1c_6 ↔ c_6c_1 (square); (iv) non-planar neutral: c_1c_6 ↔ c_3c_8, c_1+c_6 = c_3+c_8. All four classes are used in the proof (p. 22: "not clear that any of these are necessary").
- FCC elementary move (pp. 15–16): any d_i is incident to four triangular plaquettes (four pairs (d_j,d_k) with d_i = d_j + d_k); replacing d_i by d_jd_k is a positive move (+1 edge), the reverse is negative (−1); no neutral moves.
- p. 3 Table 1 summary: minimal lengths and populations for 12 knot types in SC, BCC, FCC (see Table 1; numerical results from GAS).
- p. 22–23, GAS implementation: P_+ = β_N a_+(φ_n)/(a_−(φ_n) + a_0(φ_n) + β_N a_+(φ_n)), P_0 = a_0(φ_n)/(same denominator), P_− = a_−(φ_n)/(same denominator); weight W(φ) = [(a_−(φ_0)+a_0(φ_0)+β_{ℓ(φ_0)}a_+(φ_0)) / (a_−(φ_L)+a_0(φ_L)+β_{ℓ(φ_L)}a_+(φ_L))] · Π_{n=0}^{|φ|−1} β_{ℓ(φ_n)}^{σ(n,n+1)} (Eq. 3; σ = −1 for a positive move, +1 for a negative move); GAS theorem: ⟨W⟩_N/⟨W⟩_M = p_N(K)/p_M(K) (Eq. 4), an approximate enumeration algorithm. β_N estimated recursively during simulation.
- p. 24 (BFACF-style implementation, BCC): choose an edge with probability 1/|ω|; there are 12 possible moves on a BCC edge and 4 on an FCC edge; at most one is negative. Move classes (positive, neutral, negative): (12,0,0), (11,1,0), (10,2,0), (9,2,1), (9,3,0), (8,4,0), (7,4,1), (6,6,0). Boltzmann relation P_+ = e^{−2β}P_−. Constraints: 12P_+ ≤ 1; 11P_+ + P_0 ≤ 1; 10P_+ + 2P_0 ≤ 1; 9P_+ + 3P_0 ≤ 1; 9P_+ + 2P_0 + P_− ≤ 1; 8P_+ + 4P_0 ≤ 1; 7P_+ + 4P_0 + P_− ≤ 1; 6P_+ + 6P_0 ≤ 1. Assuming P_+ ≤ P_0 ≤ P_−, maximising gives **Eq. (5):** P_+ = e^{−2β}/(3(1+3e^{−2β})), P_0 = (1+e^{−2β})/(6(1+3e^{−2β})), P_− = 1/(3(1+3e^{−2β})); P_0 = ½(P_+ + P_−).
- p. 25 (FCC): edge has either four positive moves or three positive plus one negative. Constraints 4P_+ ≤ 1, 3P_+ + P_− ≤ 1, P_0 = 0; Boltzmann relation P_+ = e^{−β}P_−. **Eq. (6) (as printed):** P_+ = e^{−β}/(1+3e^{−2β}), P_− = 1/(1+3e^{−β}). ⚠ The printed denominator of P_+ is 1+3e^{−2β}, but the constraint 3P_+ + P_− = 1 with P_+ = e^{−β}P_− gives P_+ = e^{−β}/(1+3e^{−β}); the paper's P_+ formula appears inconsistent with its own derivation.
- p. 25, Eq. (7)–(9): transition probabilities P_{ω→τ} = P_+/|ω|, P_−/|ω|, P_0/|ω| for positive, negative, neutral moves; detailed balance |ω|P_{ω→τ} = |τ|P_{τ→ω} (Eq. 8); limiting distribution P_β(ω) = |ω|e^{β|ω|}/Σ_τ |τ|e^{β|τ|} (restricted to fixed knot type K; = Σ n p_n(K)e^{βn} in the denominator) (Eq. 9).
- p. 23: the SC BFACF moves are not irreducible on fixed-endpoint walks unless the endpoints are ≥ 2 apart [8]; authors "believe that a similar result will hold" for BCC/FCC but have not investigated.

## Methods / algorithms
1. Define BFACF-style elementary moves on BCC (12 plaquettes per edge; 2 positive/negative classes, 2 neutral classes, Figs 4–5) and FCC (single ±1 move, Fig. 8) (pp. 7–9, 15–16).
2. Stretch polygons (translate line segments/isolated vertices across a maximal plane using the moves, Figs 6, 9–12) (pp. 9–12, 16–19).
3. Subdivide to make polygons contact free (Lemmas 3, 8).
4. Sweep contact-free polygons into an SC-isotopic sublattice L (BCC: basis {c_1,c_3,c_4,c_5,c_7,c_8}; FCC: {d_1,d_3,d_5,d_7,d_9,d_11}) (Thm 4, Lemma 9).
5. In L the elementary moves induce usual SC BFACF moves (BCC: directly; FCC: two FCC moves per BFACF move) and Theorem 1 applies ⇒ Theorems 6, 10.
6. Implementation (pp. 22–25): (a) GAS sampling with atmosphere sizes and weights (Eqs. 2–4); (b) Metropolis BFACF-style algorithm: initialise a polygon of the desired knot type and parameter β; choose edge with prob. 1/|ω_n|; list possible moves; perform a given positive/neutral/negative move with probabilities P_+/P_0/P_−; reject if not self-avoiding or no move; repeat (steps 1–5 on p. 25). Sampling distribution Eq. (9).
7. Data (Table 1) produced with the GAS implementation; details "published elsewhere" (p. 22).
