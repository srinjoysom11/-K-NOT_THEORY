## Abstract
We give a simple example showing that a knot or link diagram that lies in the ℤ² lattice is not necessarily the projection of a lattice stick knot or link in the ℤ³ lattice, and we give a necessary and sufficient condition for when a knot or link diagram that lies in the ℤ² lattice is in fact the projection of a lattice stick knot or link.

## Notation and conventions
- ℤ³ lattice: "the graph in ℝ³ where the vertices are the points with integer coefficients, and the edges are unit length and parallel to the coordinate axes" (p. 1). Lattice stick knots and links = knots and links contained in the ℤ³ lattice (p. 2). Notes some authors say "cubic lattice" and "step" for an edge (p. 1).
- x-edge, y-edge, z-edge: an edge of ℤ² or ℤ³ parallel to that axis. "A z-stick in the ℤ³ lattice is a line segment that is the union of finitely many z-edges; for convenience, we will sometimes consider vertices in the ℤ² lattice to be trivial z-sticks." (p. 2)
- π_xy: orthogonal projection ℝ³ → xy-plane (p. 2).
- **Definition 2.1 (p. 3).** (1) K is **proper** if the inverse image under projection onto the xy-plane of any point in the diagram that is not a vertex of ℤ² is a single point; the inverse image of a non-crossing vertex is a single z-stick (possibly trivial); the inverse image of a crossing vertex is two z-sticks (possibly trivial). (2) For proper K with projection R and vertex a: π_xy^{-1}(a) is two z-sticks; the higher is the **upper z-stick** of K over a, the other the **lower z-stick**.
- **Definition 2.2 (p. 3).** "A **lattice diagram of a knot or link** is a diagram of a knot or link, such that the diagram is contained in the ℤ² lattice, and that all crossings in the knot or link diagram are at vertices of the ℤ² lattice."
- **Definition 2.3 (p. 4).** "A **Celtic configuration** is a subset of a lattice diagram of a knot or link that is equivalent to either of the configurations in Figure 5, where the crossings in the figure are at adjacent vertices in the ℤ² lattice."
- Lattice graph = subgraph of ℤ²; lattice arc / lattice simple closed curve = lattice graph that is a path / cycle graph. Adjacent = joined by an edge of G (p. 5).
- **Definition 3.1 (p. 5).** (1) deleted-square free: no three edges that are part of a unit square in ℤ². (2) opposing / nearby neighbors of v: a, b adjacent to v with ⟨v,a⟩, ⟨v,b⟩ parallel / perpendicular. (3) corner: v adjacent to precisely two vertices which are nearby neighbors. (4) 2-near regular: degree of every vertex at most 2.
- **Definition 3.2 (p. 5).** x-strand / y-strand of R at crossing c: union of two x-edges / y-edges of R with c as endpoint; c is an x-crossing / y-crossing if the upper strand is the x-strand / y-strand (Fig. 6).
- **Definition 3.3 (p. 5).** Crossing graph CG_R: lattice graph with a vertex at every crossing of R and an edge between any two vertices adjacent in ℤ².
- **Definition 3.4 (p. 6).** c is a **problem crossing** if, as a vertex of CG_R, it has a pair of nearby neighbors (**bad neighbors** of c) which both have the opposite crossing type as c.
- **Definition 3.7 (p. 8).** Problem crossing graph PG_R: subgraph of CG_R with a vertex at every problem crossing and an edge between any two vertices that are bad neighbors of each other.
- **Definitions 4.1–4.3, 4.5, 4.6 (pp. 9–11):** lattice map f: C → ℝ³ (C a 2-regular graph; vertices → ℤ³ vertices, edges → single ℤ³ edges); x/y/z-edges and maximal x/y/z-arcs; **lattice pre-image** for R: (a) π_xy(f(C)) = R; (b) preimage under π_xy∘f of a non-vertex point is a single point; (c) of a non-crossing vertex is a single z-arc (possibly degenerate); (d) of a crossing is two z-arcs, one meeting only x-edges, the other only y-edges. x-facing / y-facing z-arc and z-stick; x-pre-strand / y-pre-strand; crossing v **resolved w.r.t. f** if (a) x-facing and y-facing z-sticks at v disjoint and (b) for an x-crossing (y-crossing) every point of the x-facing z-stick has larger (smaller) z-value than every point of the y-facing z-stick. (x,p)-lift at v: remove x-facing z-stick at v and the z-sticks at the x-strand endpoints s, t, move the two x-edges of the x-pre-strand to z-value p, re-add z-sticks; x-lift, (y,p)-lift, y-lift, lift; a lift is **proper** if afterwards v is resolved. Lift at v **compatible** with w if it did not change w from resolved to not resolved; **backwards compatible** if compatible with all other crossings.
- Prior to modification every crossing of a lattice diagram has z-value 0 (p. 10).

## Numbered statements
**Theorem 2.4 (p. 4).** Let R be a lattice diagram of a knot or link. Then R is the projection of a proper lattice stick knot or link if and only if R does not have a Celtic configuration. [proved here, Section 4; knots and links]
Proof idea (p. 9): "we start with a lattice diagram of a knot or link, and we then modify it one crossing at a time, so that after each modification, the strands at that crossing do not intersect, and such that the crossings that were previously modified remain with their strands not intersecting; … we accomplish [this] by doing the modification at the crossings in a specific order."

**Remark 3.5 (p. 6).** (stated without proof) Let R be a lattice diagram of a knot or link. (1) Let d be a crossing of R. Then d is not a problem crossing if and only if at least one pair of opposing neighbors of d has the property that each of the two opposing neighbors either is not a crossing or is a crossing having the same crossing type as d. (2) Suppose that four crossings of R are the vertices of a unit square in the ℤ² lattice. These crossings form a Celtic configuration if and only if each crossing of the four is a problem crossing and its two adjacent crossings among the four are among its bad neighbors.

**Lemma 3.6 (pp. 6–7).** Let R be a lattice diagram of a knot or link.
(1) Suppose that two problem crossings in R are adjacent. Then either the two problem crossings have the same crossing type and neither is a bad neighbor of the other, or they have the opposite crossing type and each is a bad neighbor of the other.
(2) Suppose that four crossings in R are the vertices of a unit square. If at least two of these four vertices is a problem crossing such that its two adjacent crossings among the four are among its bad neighbors, then all four of the crossings are problem crossings, and the four crossings form a Celtic configuration.
(3) If R has a problem crossing with more than two bad neighbors that are problem crossings, then R has a Celtic configuration.

**Lemma 3.8 (pp. 8–9).** (proof omitted; "derived straightforwardly from Lemma 3.6, combined with the fact that a deleted-square free lattice simple closed curve must have a vertex that is not a corner") Let R be a lattice diagram of a knot or link.
(1) If two problem crossings of R are adjacent in CG_R but are not adjacent in PG_R, then neither is a bad neighbor of the other, and they have the same crossing type.
(2) If R does not have a Celtic configuration, then PG_R is 2-near regular and deleted-square free, and every component of PG_R that is a lattice simple closed curve has a vertex that is not a corner of PG_R.

**Remark 4.4 (p. 10).** Let R be a lattice diagram of a knot or link. Let f: C → ℝ³ be a lattice pre-image for R. Then the image of f is a proper lattice stick knot or link that projects onto R if and only if each crossing of R is resolved with respect to f.

## Key equations and quantitative claims outside numbered statements
- Example (p. 4): the lattice diagram R in Figure 4, "which is the knot 5_2", is not the projection of a proper lattice stick knot (cyclic z-ordering contradiction around crossings a, b, c, d).
- p. 11: if v is an x-crossing, an (x,p)-lift or (y,−p)-lift for any sufficiently large p ∈ ℕ is proper (similarly for y-crossings).
- p. 12: problem crossing a in Fig. 9 after (y,+1)-lifts at b and d: a proper lift at a would be an (x,p)-lift for p ≥ 2 (breaks b) or a (y,−q)-lift for q ≥ 1 (breaks d); no proper lift compatible with both.
- No stick numbers / lengths are computed in this paper.

## Methods / algorithms
Proof of Theorem 2.4 (pp. 12–14):
1. Necessity: a Celtic configuration gives the cyclic z-order contradiction of Fig. 4.
2. Sufficiency: view R as a lattice pre-image of itself; perform proper, backwards-compatible lifts one crossing at a time; final pre-image has all crossings resolved, hence is an embedding projecting to R (Remark 4.4).
3. Observation (1): if w adjacent to v has the same crossing type, any proper x- or y-lift at v with sufficiently large |z| is compatible with w.
4. Observation (2): a non-problem crossing has a backwards-compatible lift (lift the upper strand in the direction of same-type opposing neighbors).
5. Problem crossings first, one component M of PG_R at a time (PG_R is 2-near regular by Lemma 3.8(2)): isolated vertex — any proper lift; lattice arc v1…vn — lift at v1 perpendicular to ⟨v1,v2⟩, then at v_i perpendicular to ⟨v_{i−1}, v_i⟩; simple closed curve — start after a non-corner vertex w_m so the last lift is also perpendicular to ⟨w_m, w_1⟩.
6. Then non-problem crossings one at a time via Observation (2).
