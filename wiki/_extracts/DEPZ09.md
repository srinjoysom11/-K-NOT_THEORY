## Abstract
For a knot or link K, let L(K) be the ropelength of K and Cr(K) be the crossing number of K. In this paper, we show that there exists a constant a > 0 such that L(K) ≤ a Cr(K) ln⁵(Cr(K)) for any K. This result shows that the upper bound of the ropelength of any knot is almost linear in terms of its minimum crossing number, and is a significant improvement over the best known upper bound established previously, which is of the form L(K) ≤ O(Cr(K)^{3/2}). The approach used to establish this result is in fact more general. In fact, we prove that any 4-regular plane graph of n vertices can be embedded into the cubic lattice with an embedding length at most of the order O(n ln⁵(n)), while preserving its topology. Since a knot diagram can be treated as a 4-regular plane graph. More specifically, Although the main idea in the proof uses a divide-and-conquer technique, the task is highly non-trivial because the topology of the knot (or of the graph) must be preserved by the embedding.

## Notation and conventions
- **Ropelength convention: LENGTH / RADIUS (disk thickness).** "we use the so called disk thickness introduced in [18]: Let K be a C² knot. A number r > 0 is said to be nice if for any distinct points x, y on K, D(x,r) ∩ D(y,r) = ∅, where D(x,r), D(y,r) are the discs of radius r centered at x and y which are normal to K. The disk thickness of K is defined to be t(K) = sup{r : r is nice}." (p. 2). So thickness = radius of a normal disk; unit thickness = tube of radius 1. "A thick realization K of K is a knot of unit thickness ... The ropelength L(K) of K is the infimum of the length of K taken over all thick realizations" (Def. 1.1, p. 2). Lattice link: "the ropelength of a knot K is bounded above by twice the length of the lattice embedding" (p. 51), consistent with radius 1/2 tubes around unit lattice edges. "the results ... also hold for other thickness definitions with a suitable change in the constant coefficient" (p. 2). No explicit numerical value of the constant a is given anywhere in the paper.
- Cr(K) = minimum crossing number; L(K) = ropelength (p. 1).
- Plane graph, plane ambient isotopy, topological equivalence, cyclic edge-order (pp. 3–4); 3D VNP-isotopy (vertex-neighborhood preserving) (p. 6); weighted graph (weights of vertices, edges, faces sum to 1), α-cycle cut, size of cycle cut = number of vertices in γ (Def. 2.7, 2.8, p. 6). d = maximal face size.
- BR-partition: vertices partitioned into blue V^B and red V^R, no edge between two blue vertices (Def. 3.1, p. 7); BR-component G(V*_M); triangulated = every face a triangle or digon; BRT-graph = every BR-component triangulated (Def. 3.2, p. 7); cut-cycle / normal cut-cycle; subdividing by vertex-cut (Def. 3.5) or edge-cut (Def. 3.7); gluing instruction, orientation vector (Def. 3.9).
- Standard weight W_s(G) = number of red vertices |V^R| (p. 17). Balanced subdivision (Def. 4.1): min{W_s(G_1), W_s(G_2)} ≥ W_s(G)/6, and for an edge-cut the normal cycle length (number of red vertices) ≤ c W_s(G). Terminal BRT-graph: W_s below threshold W_0 (Def. 4.5). G(i,j): j-th graph at depth i of the balanced recursive subdivision sequence.
- Standard 3D-embedding (Def. 5.1, p. 26), pre-determined standard 3D-embedding (Def. 5.2), grid-like embedding (Def. 5.4, p. 27): (i) red vertices are lattice points in z = 0 inside "red squares" of size w×l, w,l ≥ 3; (ii) contained in a box B = R×[0,t], blue vertices on the top, blue bands/triangles/squares; (iii) edges outside red squares/blue triangles are axis-parallel paths with integer z for horizontal segments; (iv) red-red edges on the lattice except inside red squares; (v) isotopic to a standard 3D-embedding by a VNP-isotopy.
- Turning number tr(e) of an edge = number of different horizontal gridlines needed; T(G^g, B) = max over red-blue edges of tr(e) (Def. 8.1, p. 43).
- A(n) (Def. 8.6, p. 48): minimum p such that any rectangle R with aspect ratio ≥ 1/6 and area ≥ p holds a grid-like embedding of any BRT-graph with n red vertices (max red degree 12) in a box R×[0, c_h], c_h ≤ 2⌈ln(n)/ln(6/5)⌉ + 1.

- **Lemma 2.1 (p. 4).** Let G_1, G_2 be two isomorphic plane graphs with φ: G_1 → G_2 the isomorphism. If for each vertex v of G_1 the cyclic edge-order of the incident edges e_1,…,e_j around v is identical to the cyclic edge-order of φ(e_1),…,φ(e_j) around φ(v), then there exists a plane isotopy Ψ with Ψ(G_1,1) = φ(G_1) = G_2; i.e. G_1 and G_2 are topologically equivalent. (Proof left as an exercise: induction on the order of the graph.)
- **Lemma 2.2 (p. 4).** Let R be a rectangle and x_1,…,x_n, y_1,…,y_n 2n distinct points in R. Then there exist n disjoint (piecewise smooth) curves τ_1,…,τ_n with τ_j from x_j to y_j; moreover for any given simply connected region Ω in the interior of R containing none of the points, the curves can be chosen to avoid Ω (many such regions allowed if disjoint).
- **Lemma 2.3 (p. 4).** Let G be a plane graph with vertices v_1,…,v_n and R a rectangle disjoint from G with n distinct points y_1,…,y_n chosen. Then there exists a plane isotopy Ψ with Ψ(G,1) ⊂ R and Ψ(v_j,1) = y_j; for any simply connected region Ω in the interior of R containing none of the y_j, Ψ can be chosen to keep Ω fixed.
- **Lemma 2.4 (p. 5).** Let G be a plane graph drawn in a rectangle R containing n disjoint non-closed simple curves γ_1,…,γ_n not meeting the vertices of G. For any k vertices v_1,…,v_k of G and any k distinct points x_1,…,x_k in R not on the γ_j, there exists a plane isotopy that is the identity outside a small neighborhood of R and on the γ_j taking v_j to x_j.
- **Lemma 2.5 (p. 5).** Let G be a plane graph with vertices v_1,…,v_n in a rectangle R×{0} and y_1,…,y_n distinct points in R×{t}, t>0. If y_j is joined to v_j by a curve ν_j strictly increasing in z, with the ν_j disjoint, then there is a 3D level-preserving isotopy Ψ, identity in z ≥ t and outside a small neighborhood of R×[0,t], with Ψ(ν_j,1) a vertical segment ending at y_j.
- **Remark 2.6 (p. 5).** If ν_j are only non-decreasing in z, the conclusion of Lemma 2.5 holds without the level-preserving requirement; Ψ(·,0) is identity and Ψ(G,1) is topologically equivalent to G.

Then there exists a (2/3)-cycle cut of size at most 2√(2⌊d/2⌋ n), where d is the maximal face size of G. (n = number of vertices; √ confirmed on p. 6 image.) *Quoted.*

Then either there exists a (2/3)-cycle cut of size 2√(2⌊d/2⌋ n), or there exists a cut vertex v of G such that each connected component in G∖v has total weight ≤ 2/3. *Quoted.*

## Key equations and quantitative claims outside numbered statements
- p. 1–2: main bound L(K) ≤ a Cr(K) ln⁵(Cr(K)) for every knot/link K (rigorous; constant a unspecified). Previous best general upper bound O(Cr^{3/2}) [13].
- p. 51: lattice embedding length ≤ volume of the surrounding box; ropelength ≤ 2 × lattice length; embedding of the knot adds 1 to the box height when crossings are recovered (rerouting through z = 0.5, Fig. 28).
- p. 52: "In [14], a numerical study suggests the possibility of a ropelength upper bound of the form O(Cr(K) ln²(Cr(K)))" (numerical/conjectural); "the only possible further improvement on Theorem 8.3 is to reduce the power on the ln(Cr(K)) term" since knots with O(Cr(K)) exist.
- Constants appearing: balanced subdivision fraction 1/6 to 5/6; max red degree 12 (Lemma 4.8); blue vertices ≤ 3W_0 per terminal graph (g = 12 in Lemma 4.2, so |V^B| ≤ 3|V^R|); terminal graphs have ≤ 4W_0 vertices (p. 46); red squares ≥ 3×3; c_t = 17; c_r depends on W_0 and g; recursion depth ≤ c_r ln n; box height ≤ 2⌈ln n/ln(6/5)⌉ + 1.
- Area recursion A'(n) ≤ d n (ln n)⁴ and height O(ln n) ⇒ volume O(n ln⁵ n).

## Methods / algorithms
Pure-math construction (divide and conquer).
1. (§2, pp. 3–6) Plane-graph preliminaries; Miller's planar cycle-separator theorems (Thm 2.9/2.10) give 2/3-balanced cycle cuts of size ≤ 2√(2⌊d/2⌋ n).
2. (§3, pp. 7–17) BRT-graphs (triangulated BR-components), cut-vertices are blue; subdivide by vertex-cut at a blue cut vertex or by edge-cut along a normal cut-cycle (push-off γ'); the cycle's vertices become new blue vertices; gluing instructions encode how to reconstruct (Lemma 3.10).
3. (§4, pp. 17–22) Balanced subdivisions with standard weight = number of red vertices (Lemma 4.3 via Miller with non-standard weights); recursive subdivision depth ≤ c_r ln n (Thm 4.6). Knot diagram D (minimum projection, n crossings, 4-regular plane graph) triangulated to G with max degree 12 (Lemma 4.8); all vertices red initially.
4. (§5, pp. 26–28) Standard 3D-embedding in a box R×[0,t]: blue squares, blue bands and blue triangles rising to the top of the box; grid-like embedding (Def. 5.4) as a nearly-lattice version.
5. (§6, pp. 28–36) Grid-like embedding of terminal graphs directly (Lemma 6.1); reconnection of G_1^g, G_2^g in boxes B_1, B_2 placed side by side with a connecting rectangle; new red-red edges across it placed on newly created gridlines; new blue squares/bands/triangles at the new blue vertices; linear orders recovered from gluing instructions (cases 5', Fig. 17–18; Figs 19–20 stretching space).
6. (§7, pp. 37–42) Topology preservation: grid-like embedding of G_0 is VNP-isotopic to a standard 3D-embedding (Lemma 7.1), via hook-removing isotopy along blue bands (Figs 22–26).
7. (§8, pp. 43–51) Count gridlines through turning numbers (Def. 8.1, Thm 8.2: ≤ +17 per level); each reconnection adds ≤ c ln(W_s)√(W_s) gridlines to each side; rectangles with aspect ratio ≥ 1/6 and recursive area bound A'(n) (Thm 8.7) ⇒ A(n) ≤ d n (ln n)⁴ (Thm 8.8); height ≤ c_h ln n ⇒ volume O(n ln⁵ n); recover crossings by rerouting (Fig. 28).
