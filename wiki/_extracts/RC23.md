## Abstract
In this paper we present a new Monte Carlo Search (MCS) algorithm for finding the ground state energy of proteins in the HP-model. We also compare it briefly to other MCS algorithms not usually used on the HP-model and provide an overview of the algorithms used on HP-model. The algorithm presented in this paper does not beat state of the art algorithms, see PERM (Hsu and Grassberger 2011), REMC (Thachuk, Shmygelska, and Hoos 2007) or WLRE (Wüst and Landau 2012) for better results.

## Notation and conventions
- **HP model** (p. 1): "proteins are represented as a chain of H and P residues (amino acids), the chain is then folded onto a grid, two residues can not share the same positions. The residue contacts determine the energy of a chain, usually, the reward for an H-H connection is -1, and 0 for H-P and P-P contacts in a context of minimisation". Exists in 2D and 3D versions; the experiments are on the 3D model ("Solving the 3D HP model with the NMCS requires using a level of 4 at least", p. 2). The lattice type is not named explicitly in the paper (⚠ presumably cubic; not stated).
- **−E\*** (Table 1, p. 4): the "speculated lowest energy state" of each benchmark molecule, given as a positive number (i.e. energy −E\*).
- **M_{c-state}** (p. 2): "the legal moves available from the state current-state". **G_{M_{c-state}}**: "the immediate gains of each legal moves available from the state current-state".
- **b**: playout bias factor; **l**: nesting level; **p**: number of playouts used to evaluate a state; **r** (ratio): "the ratio to the threshold a state will be pruned on" (p. 3).
- **tr**: list of pruning thresholds indexed by the number of moves already played (c-st.nbplay) (Alg. 3, p. 3).
- Chain growth = self-avoiding-walk-like residue-by-residue construction (p. 2).

## Key equations and quantitative claims outside numbered statements
- Biased-growth move probability (p. 2): P(m) = exp(G[m]·b) / Σ_{i∈M} exp(G[i]·b), softmax over legal moves M with immediate gain G and bias factor b.
- Playout immediate-gain used in experiments (p. 3): +1 for any legal H-H connection, −0.2 penalty for H-P connections ("to incite the biased growth to keep a maximum of H mers open to future connections, we did not experiment on that variable").
- Benchmark: 10 molecules with 48 mers from Thachuk–Shmygelska–Hoos 2007 / Hsu–Grassberger 2011 (p. 3).
- Protocol: LNMCS with 150 s timeout; if lowest known energy not found, restart until found (p. 3). Times in minutes on a 3.50GHz Intel core i5-6600K CPU (p. 4).
- Molecule 4 parameters: level 4, #eval playouts 10, pruning ratio 0.97, playout bias 20; threshold based on the mean of the evaluation playouts (p. 4).
- Other molecules: level 5, #eval playouts 20, pruning ratio 0.9, playout bias 20; threshold based on the best average from a batch of evaluation playouts (p. 4).
- Molecules 6 and 7: "performed very poorly … not able to gather enough data to compute the statistics"; but LNMCS "could attain the second best energy level very reliably in less than 150s in half of the launches with both molecules" (p. 4).
- Molecule 9: reached second-best level easily, but optimal "every 2 hours approximately" (p. 4).
- Plain NMCS found the optimal value (−32) on molecule 1 "in less than 10mn" (p. 4).
- NMCS vs LNMCS comparison: level 5, ratio 0.9 (LNMCS), playout bias 20, 20 runs, 150 s timeout, molecule 1 (p. 4). Claim: LNMCS reaches lowest-energy conformations "4 times as often"; lowest-energy conformations are sparser than second-lowest by "about an order of magnitude or two" (p. 4). ⚠ see Extraction notes.
- NRPA and GNRPA (bias 20) on molecule 1: did not reach optimum; "-28 or -29 for the GNRPA when -32 is the best known" (p. 5). Move representation: number of residues already placed and direction.
- Greedy BFS with playouts on molecule 1: converges to −29 (best known −32), then improves very little (p. 5).
- UCT on molecule 1: without biased growth ≈ −18 ("about the same scores as a single biased playout"); with biased growth around −28/−29 (p. 5).

## Methods / algorithms
1. **Biased growth playout (Alg. 1, p. 2):** from c-state, while not terminal: compute gains of legal moves; choose move by softmax with gains·b; play; append to seq. Return score(state), seq. Chain-growth (residues added next to the previous one, like a SAW).
2. **NMCS (Alg. 2, p. 3; Cazenave 2009):** level 0 = playout. At level l, at each step, for every legal move run NMCS at level l−1 on the child; keep best score and best sequence (≥ comparison); play the move from the best sequence at the current ply; repeat until terminal.
3. **Lazy NMCS (Alg. 3, p. 3):** as NMCS, but each child n-st is first evaluated by the mean es of p biased playouts (lines 11–14); global threshold list tr indexed by nbplay is extended (lines 15–17) and updated to the max of evaluations seen (lines 18–20; "it is possible to use a mean, a median or a max … here we use the max as it gave the best results"); if es < ratio·tr[nbplay] the child is searched only at level 0 (pruned), else at level l−1 (lines 21–25). Best-sequence bookkeeping as in NMCS.
   - Stated flaw (p. 3): only one FOR loop over moves, so evaluation is incomplete when deciding whether to prune first branches; "experiments were made with this 'prototype' version".
4. **Other baselines tried on molecule 1 only (pp. 4–5):** NMCS, NRPA/GNRPA, Greedy best-first search with playout evaluation, UCT.
5. Stopping/verification: restart LNMCS every 150 s until the lowest known energy is reached; statistics = mean time and interquartile range (p. 3–4).

## Stated limitations, open problems, future work
- "The algorithm presented in this paper does not beat state of the art algorithms" (abstract, p. 1); conclusion: "likely does not outperform the state of the art algorithms, it has the advantage to be easier to implement and may be applicable to more problems" (p. 5).
- Prototype single-loop LNMCS has incomplete evaluation when pruning first branches; "easily fixed by ulterior versions (along with other shortcomings)" (p. 3).
- Results on other MCS algorithms "only … give an idea of these algorithms' performances and do not necessarily reflect their potential on the HP model" (p. 4). "Our inability to obtain good results with NRPA does not mean it is impossible to solve this problem with it." (p. 5)
- Greedy BFS: "Pruning the search tree could improve the results of this method." (p. 5)
- "Different methods of evaluation and pruning can greatly change the performance of the algorithm, and some methods can be ineffective on a set of molecules while being capable on another set." (p. 4)
- Future work: "apply it to other problems and find ways to improve its performance" (p. 5).
