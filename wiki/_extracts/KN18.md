## Abstract
The lattice stick number s_L(L) of a link L is defined to be the minimal number of straight line segments required to construct a stick presentation of L in the cubic lattice. Hong, No and Oh [5] found a general upper bound s_L(K) ≤ 3c(K) + 2. A rational link can be represented by a lattice presentation with exactly 4 z-sticks. An n-circuit is the disjoint union of n arcs in the lattice plane Z². An n-circuit presentation is an embedding obtained from the n-circuit by connecting each n pair of vertices with one line segment above the circuit. By using a 2-circuit presentation, we can easily find the lattice presentation with exactly 4 z-sticks. In this paper, we show that an upper bound for the lattice stick number of rational p/q-links realized with exactly 4 z-sticks is 2p + 6. Furthermore it is 2p + 5 if L is a 2-component link.

## Notation and conventions
- Cubic lattice Z³ = (R×Z×Z) ∪ (Z×R×Z) ∪ (Z×Z×R); s_L(L) = least number of sticks to make L in Z³ (p. 2). Stick = straight segment (any integer length), not a unit step; no ropelength involved.
- x-stick, x-level k = yz-plane with x-coordinate k; similarly y, z (p. 2).
- n-circuit: disjoint union of n arcs in the lattice plane Z², endpoints labelled v_1, v_1′, …, v_n, v_n′ with v_i = (x_i, y_i), v_i′ = (x_i′, y_i′) (p. 2). Regular circuit: for all i, j: x_i = x_i′ or y_i = y_i′; and [x_i, x_i′] ∩ [x_j, x_j′] = ∅ or [y_i, y_i′] ∩ [y_j, y_j′] = ∅, if i ≠ j (printed "i = j" in the text layer, ⚠ evidently ≠) (p. 2).
- n-circuit presentation: embedding obtained from the n-regular circuit by connecting each pair v_i, v_i′ with one line segment above the circuit (p. 2; Fig. 2 shows a 3-circuit presentation of the (4,3)-torus knot).
- Rational tangle T(a_1, …, a_n) (Fig. 3) is a rational p/q-tangle if p/q = a_n + 1/(a_{n−1} + 1/(… + 1/(a_2 + 1/a_1))) (p. 3). Conway's theorem: p/q-tangle ≅ p′/q′-tangle iff p/q = p′/q′ (p. 4). Rational p/q-link = closure joining top endpoints together and bottom endpoints together (Fig. 5) (p. 4). Pillowcase form: arcs of the tangle lie on the boundary; t (resp. s) = number of gaps between arcs across top/bottom (resp. each side); (t,s)-form (p. 4-5). Vertical/horizontal twist; vertical/horizontal crossing (p. 4).
- p, q coprime in the main theorem (p. 6).

## Numbered statements
- **Theorem 1 (p. 3).** An upper bound for the lattice stick number of rational p/q-links realized with exactly 4 z-sticks is 2p + 6. Furthermore it is 2p + 5 if L is a 2-component link. *Proved here (p. 6-8).* Proof idea (p. 8): build a regular 2-circuit from the (p,q)-form diagram (4p + 4 sticks), then save one stick by re-routing a path, push P2 down to z-level −1 so that it needs at most two sticks (a single stick for a 2-component link).
- **Proposition 2 (p. 5).** If the (t,s)-form is equivalent to the rational p/q-tangle then (t, s) = (p, q). *Result introduced by Cromwell [3]; proved here (p. 5-6).* Proof idea: adding a vertical crossing turns a (t,s)-form into a (t, t+s)-form, adding a horizontal crossing gives (t+s, s); starting from T_0 = (0,1)-form and applying a_1 horizontal, a_2 vertical, … crossings (giving (a_1,1), (a_1, a_1a_2+1), …) and inducting on k shows the (t_k,s_k)-form is a t_k/s_k tangle (k odd) or, after reflection, the (s_k,t_k)-form is s_k/t_k (k even); uniqueness of rational tangles gives p = t, q = s.
- No other numbered statements.

## Key equations and quantitative claims outside numbered statements
- p. 1: stick number (ordinary) of the trefoil is 6.
- p. 2 (quoted): Huh–Oh [8,9]: exactly two nontrivial knots have s_L ≤ 14: s_L(3_1) = 12, s_L(4_1) = 14. Huang–Yang [7]: s_L(5_1) = s_L(5_2) = 16. Adams et al. [1]: s_L(8_20) = s_L(8_21) = s_L(9_46) = 18, s_L(4^2_1) = 13, s_L(T_{p,p+1}) = 6p for p ≥ 2, via s_L(K) ≥ 6b(K) (Janse van Rensburg–Promislow [10]). Diao–Ernst [4]: s_L(K) ≥ 3√(c(K)+1) + 3 for nontrivial K. Hong–No–Oh [5]: s_L(K) ≤ 3c(K)+2, and ≤ 3c(K) − 4 for non-alternating prime K. Hong–No–Oh [6]: s_L(2^2_1) = 8, s_L(2^2_1♯2^2_1) = s_L(6^3_2) = s_L(6^3_3) = 12, s_L(4^2_1) = 13, s_L(5^2_1) = 14, any other non-split link ≥ 15. (Link names printed garbled in the text layer.)
- p. 2: a nontrivial knot in the cubic lattice must have at least 4 z-sticks (stated as known); only 2-bridge links and the trivial knot can be represented with exactly 4 z-sticks.
- p. 3: consistency checks: 3_1 = rational 3/1 and 5_1 = rational 5/1, Theorem 1 gives 12 and 16, "exactly same with their lattice stick number"; the bound for 4^2_1 is 13 "also exactly same with its lattice stick number" (⚠ 4^2_1 as 2-component rational link needs p = 4: 2·4 + 5 = 13, consistent).
- p. 3: for a rational (p,1)-link the bound is 2p + 6, crossing number is p, general bound 3p + 2; so the new result is better when p > 4. "The general upper bound of lattice stick number is better than our result [for rational links in general]. However, for some cases, our result is better."
- p. 8: the regular 2-circuit has 2p vertical and 2p − 2 horizontal segments; each of P_1, P_2 has p vertical segments and 2p − 1 segments; 2-circuit presentation uses 4p + 4 sticks; one stick saved by re-routing the path through (p,p,0), (p,p,1), (0,p,1), (0,p,0), (0,−2q,0), (2q,−2q,0) to (p,p,0), (p,p,2), (p,−2q,2), (2q,−2q,2), (2q,−2q,0); then P_2 pushed to z-level −1 and replaced by ≤ 2 sticks (Fig. 9(b)); a single stick for 2-component links (Fig. 9(c)). ⚠ The paper does not itemise the arithmetic leading from 4p+4−1 to 2p+6; transcribed as printed. Presumably P_2's 2p−1 sticks → 2 gives 4p+4−1−(2p−1)+2 = 2p+6 (my reconstruction, not printed).
- No numerical/computational results.

## Methods / algorithms
Pure-math construction (p. 6-8).
1. Take rational p/q-link L (p, q coprime); by Prop. 2 draw it as the (p,q)-form: central segments of slope ±p/q in the square A(0,0), B(0,−1), C(1,−1), D(1,0); l_1 connects A, D; l_2 connects B, C (Fig. 7(a)).
2. Apply the linear transformation with matrix [[p, p], [−q, q]] so all central segments are horizontal or vertical (Fig. 7(b)) (matrix entries read from text layer: "p p / −q q").
3. Move horizontal segments: for i = 1..q move the segment between (p−i, p−i) and (p+i, p−i) to the one between (p−i, p+i) and (p+i, p+i); similarly for i = 1..q move the segment between (q−i, −q+i) and (q+i, −q+i) to the one between (q−i, −q−i) and (q+i, −q−i); extend vertical segments to reconnect (Fig. 8(a)).
4. The remaining p − q − 1 horizontal segments intersecting vertical lines: replace the segment between (p−q−i, p−q−i) and (p+q−i, p−q−i) by a path via (p−q−i, p+q+i), (p+q+i, p+q+i), (p+q+i, p−q−i), i = 1..p−q−1 (Fig. 8(b)); not needed when p − q = 1.
5. Extend the segment (0,0)–(0,−2q) to (0,p)–(0,−2q) and (p+q, p−q)–(p+q, p+q) to (p+q, −q)–(p+q, p+q), dropping and re-attaching any horizontal segments they meet (Fig. 9(a)); v_1 = (0,p), v_1′ = (p,p), v_2 = (q,−q), v_2′ = (p+q,−q): a regular 2-circuit with 2p vertical and 2p − 2 horizontal segments.
5'. P_1 starts at v_1, P_2 is the other path; vertical segments of P_1 have even x, of P_2 odd x; each has p vertical segments and 2p − 1 segments in all.
6. Connect v_1–v_1′ by arc l_1 (one horizontal segment at z-level 1 plus two z-sticks), v_2–v_2′ by l_2 likewise: 4p + 4 sticks, exactly 4 z-sticks.
7. Reduce: re-route as above (−1 stick), push P_2 to z-level −1, replace P_2 by ≤ 2 sticks (1 for a 2-component link): 2p + 6 (resp. 2p + 5).
