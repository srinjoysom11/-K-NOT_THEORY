# Numerical ropelength values and fitted laws

Computed tight configurations are **upper bounds** on Rop(K). R-units (length / radius) throughout; every source here uses that convention ([KA23] p. 1, [KM21] p. 1, [KT25] p. 1).

## Methods and their accuracy

* Tightening codes: Pieranski's SONO (≤ 9 crossings, later Gilbert for all prime knots and links ≤ 11 on the Knot Atlas), Millett–Rawdon stochastic crankshaft, and **Ridgerunner** ([ACPR10]) — history from [KM21] p. 2–3.
* **Ridgerunner** ([ACPR10]): minimise length over polygons of thickness ≥ 1 by constrained gradient descent. The cone of thickness-non-decreasing variations is finitely generated by explicit "struts" and "kinks" (proved), giving a first-order method and a Karush–Kuhn–Tucker criterion for polygonal ropelength criticality. Runs start at 2 vertices per unit ropelength and refine to 8 (some to 74; up to ≈ 2400 vertices); 20–40 CPU hours per knot; ≥ 5 starting configurations per knot, best kept. Output: **379 almost-critical configurations — every prime knot ≤ 10 crossings and every prime link ≤ 9 crossings** — with residual < 0.01 for 375 of them and < 0.001 for 202. Full table (Rop_p polygonal, Rop smooth upper bound, residual, rank): [data/ACPR10_table3.csv](../data/ACPR10_table3.csv) (names assigned by table order; reviewer spot-checked 60 rows against page images, all correct).
* ACPR10 highlights: 4_1 improved to 42.0887; 9_20 improved by 8.12% to 80.2219; Borromean rings 58.0070; tighter than Gilbert's Knot Atlas configurations in every case but the Hopf link (average 3.6%); trefoil 32.7435 at 2400 edges. Kinks (active curvature constraints) occur in 372 of 379 tight curves and straight segments in 338; tight 5_1 is *not* fivefold symmetric. ⚠ Internal inconsistencies flagged by the extractor: introduction "6π + 2" vs Table 1 "12π + 4"; stated average residual 0.00299 vs tabulated mean 0.00578; Rop > Rop_p for 9_28, 9_30, 10_84.
* Calibration ([KA23] p. 7): a 12-crossing chain of six Hopf-linked rings has exact ropelength 6(4π + 4) ≈ 99.4; Ridgerunner reaches 100.63 with 20 vertices per ring (+1.2%) and 102.24 with 12 (+2.9%). The authors conclude their 12-crossing values "are likely at least 1% overestimated".
* 2-fold Hopf link (two stadium curves, 16(π + 1) ≈ 66.26) is reached within 1% by Ridgerunner ([KM21] p. 4).

## Ropelength vs lattice length (*my remark*, computed 12 Nov 2026)

Dividing each knot's tight ropelength ([ACPR10], smooth Rop) by its best-known SC lattice length ([Scharein09] Table A1) over **all 249 prime knots ≤ 10 crossings**:

| statistic | Rop / Len |
|---|---|
| mean ± s.d. | 1.443 ± 0.029 |
| smallest | 3_1 1.364 (32.74 / 24), 10_140 1.368, 9_46 1.372, 5_2 1.374, 7_2 1.388 |
| largest | 10_123 1.539 (92.36 / 60), 9_31 1.512, 9_40 1.512, 10_157 1.508, 9_41 1.507 |

So tight ropelength is ≈ 1.44 × lattice length with little spread — far below the trivial bound Rop ≤ 2·Len. Both columns are upper bounds (numerical), so the ratio is not a theorem. *Use, with care:* a low ratio could mean the published lattice length is not minimal, a high one that the tight configuration is not; but the trefoil has the *lowest* ratio while Len(3_1) = 24 is proved, so the ratio also varies genuinely between knot types and is only a weak screening signal. Reproduce with `data/ACPR10_table3.csv` and `data/scharein2009_tableA1_min_step.csv`.

## 12-crossing census ([KA23], numerical)

All 2176 prime 12-crossing knots (1288 alternating, 888 non-alternating), Ridgerunner:
* mean 102.95 (s.d. 5.51); alternating 106.99 (s.d. 1.49); non-alternating 97.08 (s.d. 3.61) — non-alternating knots are ≈ 90% as long on average but at least twice as variable.
* Fitted power law over 3–12 crossings: Rop ∝ c^{0.83 ± 0.02} (previously 0.81 ± 0.03) — unweighted fits, heuristic.
* Correlations: ropelength vs hyperbolic volume 0.35 (alt.) / 0.74 (non-alt.); vs average crossing number 0.30 / 0.81; vs |Alexander(−1)| 0.39 / 0.55.
* **Writhe quantization:** alternating tight knots have writhe near multiples of 4/7 (residual s.d. 0.14); non-alternating near half-integer multiples of 4/3 (odd multiples of 2/3 for even c, even multiples for odd c; residual s.d. 0.19; uniform null 0.29). Ideal writhe correlates 0.96 with the Rasmussen s and Ozsváth–Szabó τ invariants at 12 crossings, with "no formal or heuristic explanation".
* ⚠ [KA23] p. 3 quotes the proven universal lower bound as "34.7 [Diao 2003]"; Diao's inequality 16πc ≤ Rop(Rop − 17.334) gives ≈ 23.7 at c = 3, and the best proven constant in this collection is 31.32 ([DDS04]) — treat 34.7 as a misquote unless the cited paper says otherwise.

## Large knots ([KM21], numerical + heuristic models)

* Torus knots up to 1023 crossings; satellites up to 42 crossings. **A satellite typically has ≈ 3× the ropelength of its companion** (mean ratios: link 2.98, basic 3.01, Whitehead 3.19 — Table I).
* Ensemble exponent vs crossing number: 0.81 ± 0.03 (knots), 0.81 ± 0.05 (links), 0.86 ± 0.01 (two-component composites).
* Hopf-link model: each component wraps a minimal convex hull around the others' cross-sections (Cantarella et al.: component length ≥ the minimal convex hull around unit disks). Stadium family: L = 8(N² + (π − 1)N) for the N-fold Hopf link, slope 4 per crossing at large c.
* Constraints on the C^{3/4} prefactor a_l of non-alternating knots: proven ≥ 1.105; 10_124 = T(5,3) gives a_l ≲ 12.64 (12n725 gives 12.74, [KA23]). The heuristic 8.65 was corrected to ≈ 15.7 by [KT25] (see [upper-bounds](upper-bounds.md)).
* Alternating knots: proven slope ≥ 1/59.5 ([Diao22]); 11a367 caps it at 8.15, supercoiled T(P,2) constructions at ≈ 7.6 ([KA23] p. 3).

## Pearl necklaces vs FCC lattice knots ([Klotz26pearl])

Pearl-necklace number N_P = fewest unit spheres, each tangent to its two neighbours, no overlaps, forming the knot (an equilateral polygon with edge 2 and non-adjacent vertices ≥ 2 apart). It had been speculated that N_P = N_L, the minimal FCC lattice length ([JvRR11] Table 5: trefoil 15). LLM-guided search: **no 14-sphere trefoil found**, but N_P = N_L − 1 for every knot with 5–7 crossings plus 8_19 and 10_124, and N_P = N_L − 2 for 8_1. So FCC minimal length is not the pearl-necklace number. The LLM-claimed ropelength bound ≈ 2.08 C^{3/4} in that paper is explicitly unverified by the author.

## LLM-assisted lower bounds ([Klotz26llm], status noted)

* Universal bound improved from 1.105 c^{3/4} to **2.0996 c^{3/4}** — *LLM-claimed-unverified* (numerical maximisation not certified; conditional on Buck–Simon separation inputs).
* **Rop(K) ≥ π(stick(K) − 1)** — a short derivation from the Litherland–Simon–Durumeric–Rawdon polygonal approximation (N ≤ ℓ/(πτ) + 1 edges). With Jin's stick numbers for torus knots it beats both Diao's bound and 2.0996 c^{3/4} for 41 torus knots with c < 1850.
* Quotes a preprint (Schoen, Sept 2026) claiming Rop ≥ 31.51217852 for 3 ≤ c ≤ 8 — not in this collection, unverified.
