## Statement
If P, Q are generic for the projection and `Equiv P Q`, then `inv_Q P = inv_Q Q`, for every finite
quandle Q.  `Equiv` is generated by Delta-moves, rotation, reversal, translation.  Reversal and
translation are done (`inv_reverse`, `inv_translate`).  So the work is: rotation and Delta-moves.

## C0. Rotation of the vertex list (step B, not yet done; about 300 lines)
`evs (L.rotate 1)` is `(evs L).rotate k` (k = number of events on edge 0) up to renaming the crossing
names `cid`, provided the keys (edge, par) are distinct (genericity).  Proof: (1) the unsorted event
list of `L.rotate 1` is a permutation of that of `L` with edge index `i -> i-1 mod n`
(the pair (0,j) becomes (j-1,n-1): `pairEvents` is symmetric in the two edges except for the order of
the pair and `cid`); (2) two sorted lists with distinct keys of permuted data are rotations of one
another (mergeSort is characterised by sortedness + permutation + distinct keys); (3) `colE` is
invariant under cyclic rotation of the event list and injective renaming of `cid` (a bijection of
colourings: shift the piece index).  Needed lemmas: `List.mergeSort` uniqueness, `colE_rotate`.

## C1. The abstract side: R1, R2, R3 and planar moves preserve `colE` (about 500 lines)
Define a combinatorial diagram as a cyclic list of events with `over`, `pos`, mate positions.  Moves:
* P0 (planar): adjacent events on the same strand exchanged (a crossing slides past another one
  without a change of the structure) when they belong to crossings that are not related; relabel.
* R1: insert/delete an adjacent over/under pair of the same crossing.
* R2: insert/delete two adjacent events on each of two strands, forming two crossings of opposite sign
  with the same strand over.
* R3: three crossings between three strands, one over both others, one under both others.
Each preserves `colE Q` for every quandle Q: R1 uses `x ◃ x = x`; R2 uses `left_inv`/`right_inv`;
R3 uses `self_distrib`.  Proof: explicit bijection of colourings, new pieces get the forced colours.

## C2. The geometric side (the hard part)
### C2a. A generic Delta triangle.  `T = abc`, `P' = P - [a,b] + [a,c] + [c,b]`, `T ∩ P = [a,b]`.
Genericity of the move: projected triangle non-degenerate; no vertex of P' projects into `π(T)`'s
boundary lines in a bad position (finitely many conditions, decidable).
Lemma H (height).  Let `s` be a segment (a piece of an edge of P other than [a,b]) with `π(s) ∩ π(T)`
non-empty.  The set `s ∩ π^{-1}(π T)` is a segment; the signed height of its points over the plane
of T is an affine function; it vanishes nowhere on that segment (else `s` meets T, contradicting
`T ∩ P = [a,b]`, except at the endpoints a, b which genericity excludes); hence the whole piece is
above T or below T.  (Proof: affine function on a segment with no zero has constant sign; three
lines of linear algebra over Q, `Lean42.FM` can decide the emptiness.)

### C2b. Subdivision into elementary triangles.  By Lemma H each strand piece in the prism over `π(T)`
is over or under; so the change of diagram only depends on the planar picture inside `π(T)`:
the arc `π[a,b]` is replaced by `π[a,c] ∪ π[c,b]` sweeping the region `π(T)` over strands that are
"over T" and under strands "under T".  Choose points on the sides of T (rational, avoiding finitely
many lines: the lines through pairs of projected vertices, the lines of the projected strands) so
that T is cut into triangles T_1..T_m (a fan from c) each of which is *elementary*, i.e.
`π(T_k)` contains in its interior at most one *feature* of the diagram `D(P - [a,b])`, where a feature
is a vertex of the diagram, or a crossing of two strands, and every strand piece through it
meets the boundary of `π(T_k)` transversally in two points.  Then `P → P'` is the composite of the
elementary moves `P_{k-1} -> P_k` across `T_k`.  Existence of the subdivision: finite number of
features (each feature in the interior of one small triangle after choosing mesh finer than
the minimum distance between features: explicit mesh = 1/N with N from the minimum of squared
distances of finitely many rational points, computable).  [Estimate: 800 lines.]

### C2c. Case analysis of an elementary move across `T' = a'b'c'` (edge `[a',b']` replaced by
`[a',c'],[c',b']`), strands S inside `π(T')`, each over or under T'.
(Orientation of the arc and of the strands is irrelevant, `col` counts both orientations
through `inv`; the cases are listed up to reversing the strand direction and up to exchanging the
roles of a' and b'.)
1. No feature, no strand: nothing changes (the diagram has the same events).
2. No feature, one or several strands, each crossing `π(T')` in a chord; the chord ends on two of the
   three sides.  Per strand:
   2.1 chord from `[a'b']` to `[a'c']`: the crossing with the arc moves from the edge `[a',b']`
       to `[a',c']`, same over/under (Lemma H): planar move P0 (the crossing slides along the arc past
       the vertex a'); [two sub-cases: S over/under T'; two orientations];
   2.2 chord from `[a'b']` to `[c'b']`: same with b';
   2.3 chord from `[a'c']` to `[c'b']`: old: no crossing of S with the arc, new: two crossings with
       S over (resp. under) both: R2; [S over/under; two orientations];
   2.4 several parallel chords: apply 2.1-2.3 in turn (chords do not meet: no feature);
       the order of the new events along `[a',c'],[c',b']` follows the order along the sides.
3. Feature = a vertex w of the diagram inside `π(T')`, the two edges at w continue to the
   boundary; the strand is a bent chord; its ends lie on the sides (X, Y), each in
   {a'b', a'c', c'b'}, with the vertex w on either side of the line through `[a'b']`, etc.:
   (X,Y) = same side (hairpin): 3.1 both ends on [a'b']: old 2 crossings, new 0: R2 (inverse);
   3.2 both on [a'c'] (or [c'b']): old 0, new 2: R2; 3.3 ends on two different sides: as 2.1-2.3
   (the bend does not change the order of events: only events on the arc matter), planar.
   [Orientation: 2x; over/under: 2x.]
4. Feature = a crossing x of two strands S1, S2 inside `π(T')`, four ends on the sides.  Up to
   symmetry the arc `[a'b']` and the new arc `[a',c'],[c',b']` form, with S1, S2, the configuration
   of R3 iff the crossing x lies in `π(T')` and each strand crosses `[a'b']` once and the other
   side once.  If a strand has both ends on the new sides or on the old side only, it
   decomposes: first move across the part of T' on one side (case 2.3/2.1) and then R3-free; to avoid
   this, refine the subdivision (choose points so that every strand through x meets `[a'b']`
   in one point and `[a'c']∪[c'b']` in one point, or none).  Heights: S1, S2 each over or
   under T'; (over, over), (under, under), (over, under) give A bottom, A top, A middle:
   all legal R3 configurations (S1 vs S2 heights at x are inherited).  [Cases: 3 height patterns
   x 2 chirality of x x 2 positions of c' relative to x.]
5. Feature on a side (vertex or crossing exactly on a side of T'): excluded by the choice of the
   subdivision points (genericity of the mesh); features on `[a',b']` itself are crossings of the
   arc with strands: they are events of the old diagram, treated in 2.1/2.2.
6. The vertices a', b' themselves (endpoints of the arc): the neighbouring edges of the polygon
   continue to the previous/next vertices: they are strands through a' and b'; they leave `π(T')`
   through the sides at a' (resp. b') or through the opposite side; genericity (adjacent edges
   not parallel, `okAdj`) rules out collinear continuation; the event order at a', b' changes by
   planar moves only.
Each case: (i) compute the events of P_{k-1}, P_k (a finite computation with the parameters of
Lean42/Diagram.lean); (ii) show the cyclic event lists are related by the abstract move of C1.
Estimate: 15 geometric cases x 100-200 lines each = 2000-3000 lines, the dominant cost.

## C3. Perturbation of intermediate polygons (about 1500 lines)
A chain `P = P_0, ..., P_m = Q` may leave the generic polygons.  Standard fix: for every P_k choose a
polygon P_k' generic for the projection with P_k ~ P_k' by a chain of *generic* Delta-moves
(thin triangles), and `P_k -> P_{k+1}` replaced by `P_k' -> P_k ~ P_{k+1} -> P_{k+1}'`
where the middle step is a Delta-move across a triangle that is made generic by moving apex c in
a small neighbourhood.  Needs: (1) a positive lower bound epsilon for the distance of non-adjacent
edges (minimum over finitely many pairs; explicit rational epsilon from squared distances: the
minimum of a convex function on a product of two segments: computable by FM/vertex enumeration);
(2) moving one vertex v by a vector of length < epsilon/2 is a composite of two Delta-moves whose
triangles are free (thin triangles `A v v'`, `v v' C`: free by (1) and the angle condition at A and
C); (3) the set of bad displacements (those making the polygon non-generic) is contained in finitely
many planes: a rational displacement outside them exists in every ball.  Then the perturbation of all
vertices is a sequence of single-vertex moves, each Delta-chain of two generic moves *provided* the
intermediate polygons are generic: order the vertex moves and choose displacements successively.
This is the general-position argument that we avoided for translation and for rotations.

## Update: Gauss codes (done) and the verdict on the symbolic projection direction

### Done: `Lean42/Gauss.lean` (no sorry; axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound)
The colouring count is `colG Q G = sum over oc of trace(prod of event matrices)` for a signed Gauss
code G (cyclic list of events with crossing name, over/under, sign), Q any Mathlib `Quandle`.
Proved: `colG_rotate` (cyclic rotation), `colG_rename` (injective renaming of crossing names),
`R1`, `R2` (over pair + under pair of opposite signs, either order), `R3a`/`R3b` (the three pairs in
cyclic order top-middle-bottom resp. top-bottom-middle; the sign hypothesis `sx = sy iff (B first iff C
first)` is exactly what an actual R3 move satisfies: reversing a strand reverses its pair and flips two
signs). Planar moves change nothing on Gauss codes. The count agrees with the piece count of
`Diagram.lean` on the checked examples (`DiagramCheck.lean`, `gaussOf`).
Remaining in step 1 (about 200 lines): the Gauss code of a polygon with *intrinsic* crossing names
(cid = (over edge, under edge) as point pairs) and events listed edge by edge, so that
`gauss (L.rotate 1) = (gauss L).rotate k` is a structural statement (flatMap over the rotated edge list;
`edges (L.rotate 1) = (edges L).rotate 1` follows from `edges_cons_cons`, `chain_append_pair`), and
`gauss (L.map (. + t)) = (gauss L).map (rename by translating the edge pairs)`; then `inv` for
polygons gets rotation and translation invariance from `colG_rotate`, `colG_rename`. Rotation needs
the keys (edge, parameter) to be distinct, i.e. genericity (sorting ties).

### Verdict on the symbolic direction d(eps) = (eps, eps^3, 1)
* True and useful: all degree-2 monomials of (eps, eps^3, 1) are distinct powers of eps (eps^2, eps^4,
  eps^6, eps, eps^3, 1), so every nonzero quadratic form and every nonzero linear form is a nonzero
  polynomial in eps. The non-generic directions of a simple polygon are zero sets of polynomials
  (vertex on edge: plane; triple point: quadric cone; overlapping collinear projections: plane). So
  every simple polygon is generic for d(eps), the diagram is defined for every polygon of an Equiv
  chain, and Step 4 (perturbing intermediate polygons) disappears. Signs are decided by the lowest
  coefficient; over a Delta-triangle Lemma H (constant sign of an affine height) holds in any
  ordered field. If P is strictly generic for the rational direction d0, its Gauss code for d(eps)
  equals that for d0 (all conditions are open; constant coefficients decide).
* Cost, the risk you named is real: a Delta-triangle is subdivided with rational 3D points, whose
  eps-projections are `standard + eps*...` with only three terms, and two diagram features that have the
  same d0-projection but differ at order eps (a cluster) cannot be separated by cell boundaries
  chosen at the standard level: the line through the cluster point must be chosen with the right
  first-order tilt, and clusters may be separated only at order eps^2 or eps^3. This adds a layer of
  cases (feature clusters, recursion in the order of separation) to the elementary-move analysis of C2c;
  I estimate +50 percent on C2c (1000-1500 lines) and an ordered-field infrastructure on Q[eps]
  (about 400 lines), against about 1500 lines for Step 4. Not a clear win.
* Alternative economy (recommended to evaluate first): one rational direction d generic for all
  polygons of a given chain (a rational point of the curve (e, e^3, 1) outside the finitely many bad
  roots: explicit and decidable). Then every Delta-move of the chain is a move between d-generic
  polygons, the subdivision points can be chosen generic for d (all in the rational field, no clusters),
  so Step 4 disappears and C2c keeps its 15 cases. What is lost: the invariant is then computed for
  direction d, not the fixed direction d0 of Diagram.lean, so one needs direction independence for a
  single polygon generic for both: a wall-crossing analysis along the segment from d0 to d (finitely many
  walls: vertex through an edge projection = R2-type, triple point = R3, vertex-vertex and parallel
  coincidences = planar), about 800-1200 lines, reusing the R-moves of Gauss.lean. So the
  alternative costs about the same as the symbolic route but stays over Q.
* Recommendation: take the common-generic-direction route plus wall crossing, unless the symbolic route
  is wanted for elegance. Either way, finish the polygon-to-Gauss-code bridge first.
